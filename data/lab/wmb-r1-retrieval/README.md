# WMB-R1: lexical versus hybrid retrieval on the account's own notes

Status: PREREGISTRATION, reviewed and amended before any model call of this slice (Amendment 1). Nothing below has been run on a language model. Later amendments are appended at the end, each with the reason and the commit.

## The question

The writing agent's maintainers asked (2026-11-01) whether the shared memory module needs an embedding model at all. Their server has no numpy and no torch, so a MiniLM-class embedder in ONNX would be a separate, installed, versioned dependency; the lexical memory they run now gives a candidate on about 5% of tasks at its threshold, MiniLM at cosine 0.6 on about 20%. The two numbers are not comparable (different thresholds, no judgement of whether the candidate is the right note). This slice compares lexical and embedding retrieval on equal terms, with relevance judged by a reader that does not know which method found the note.

Decision it informs: whether `memory_kernel` ships with an embedder profile on the writing agent's server for note-writing tasks (part U1), and whether question-style recall such as the persona's facts needs one later (parts U2 and U3).

## What is not claimed

One account, 234 notes, tasks written by a model from the notes themselves, one relevance judge, one embedding model. A result here is an engineering input for this deployment, not a statement about lexical versus dense retrieval in general. Thresholds are fitted in-sample and say nothing about how they would generalise. Nothing here shows that injected memory improves a note (that is E5 and the live harm check); it only measures whether the right earlier note is found, and what a false injection costs in counts.

The briefs are written from the note whose task they are: 59% of a brief's distinct content stems occur in its own note, and the shipped lexical gate fires on 27 of the 214 briefs (12.6%) and the shipped hybrid on 52 (24.3%), against the "about 5%" for live tasks quoted above (a different task set and build). Lexical findability is therefore probably overstated here, and a `LEXICAL_SUFFICIENT_FOR_GATE` verdict is a verdict for tasks of the brief kind. The relevance judge is a language model and so is the embedder, and topical similarity may raise both, so their agreement is not independent evidence of relevance; the blind audit of the decisive pairs by a second language-model reader reduces this and does not remove it. Hits are retrieval hits, not a benefit to the note, and a false injection can cost more than a hit gains: in E5, on the tasks without a candidate, always-on memory (the three nearest notes) was preferred over no memory in only 18.1% of pairs (95% interval 11.9% to 25.0%) by a pairwise judge.

## Part U1: the main part, tasks of the real kind

Tasks. Notes in the order of `parse_items` (date, then id), as in E5. Task `i` is the brief of note `i`, for `i = 20 .. 233` (E5's warm-up of 20): **214 eligible tasks**. The memory for task `i` is the notes `j < i` only. For the kernel, note `k` gets the synthetic time `day_k` 08:00 plus its rank within the day in minutes, so that earlier notes of the same day count as earlier (E5's rule; 152 of the 214 tasks have an earlier note of the same day) and the kernel's strict no-future rule agrees with it.

Methods. All use the kernel's tokenisation (lower-case stems, stop list) where they are lexical.

| id | what it is |
|---|---|
| LEX_BM25 | BM25, k1 = 1.2, b = 0.75, idf over the causal memory of the task |
| LEX_KERNEL | the kernel's lexical scoring (`memory_kernel.retrieve` with `kernel_profiles.LEX_KERNEL`: no embedding, no names, gates removed): idf-weighted coverage of the brief by the note, every earlier note that shares a stem being a candidate |
| LEX_TFIDF | cosine of sublinear tf-idf vectors |
| EMB_MINILM | cosine of MiniLM vectors of brief and note (E5's score) |
| HYB_KERNEL | the kernel's hybrid scoring (`retrieve` with `kernel_profiles.HYB_KERNEL` and ready MiniLM vectors): strength = max(coverage / tau_lex, cosine / tau_emb); the gates sit at 5% of `tau_lex` and `tau_emb`, so the score the kernel returns is 20 times the strength |
| REF_E5 | reference only: fire when the MiniLM top-1 cosine is at least 0.5722 (E5's threshold) |
| HYB_SHIPPED | exploratory only: max(coverage / 0.30, cosine / 0.57), the weights of the shipped profiles |

`tau_emb` = E5's 80th-percentile rule applied to the top-1 MiniLM cosines of the eligible tasks: the value at position int(0.8 * 214) = 171 of the ascending values (the script recomputes it and records both; 0.5722, identical to E5's, with the same top-3 candidates as E5's `prep.json` on 214 of 214 tasks). `tau_lex` = the same rule applied to the top-1 coverage of LEX_KERNEL on the causal indexes (0.2447 on the draft data). Each channel alone therefore fires on 20% of tasks at its own threshold, but HYB_KERNEL is not their union at a 20% cut: the lexical ratio has a long upper tail (95th percentile 1.60, maximum 2.99, against 1.19 and 1.60 for the cosine ratio), so the hybrid's first candidate is decided by the lexical term in 22 of 22, 32 of 43 and 41 of 65 fired tasks at q = 0.10, 0.20, 0.30. All strengths are swept (below), so the absolute values matter only for how HYB_KERNEL weighs its two channels.

Kernel calls. `kernel_profiles` in `config.json` gives the exact profiles. The shipped gates (lex_tau 0.30, min_evidence 0.8) are not used for the sweep: read literally they leave LEX_KERNEL a candidate on only 27 of 214 tasks (12.6%) and HYB_KERNEL with the calibrated taus as gates on 58 (27.1%), fewer than the 43 and 65 tasks that q = 0.20 and 0.30 need for equal exposure; their effect is read separately (operating-point readings). Every task `i` has its own kernel index built from the notes `j < i` only, so document frequencies and document count are causal; BM25, TF-IDF and the kernel share this causal idf; the kernel's `calibrate` is not used. Any kernel call with a status other than ok aborts the run. Candidates are ordered by score, then the more recent note first (E5's rule): note `j` has the id `n(9999 - j)`, so the kernel's id tie-break picks the more recent note (ids that sort by date would pick the older one).

Pool and labels. For every eligible task the pool is the union of the top 5 candidates of LEX_BM25, LEX_KERNEL, LEX_TFIDF, EMB_MINILM and HYB_KERNEL (2,065 pairs on the draft data, 9.6 per task). Each pooled pair (task, earlier note) is judged once by deepseek-flash through the judge proxy (temperature 0), blind to method and score, in an order that is a fixed shuffle of the pairs. The judge sees the brief and the earlier note and answers one word:

- `SAME`: the earlier note is about the same specific story, event, announcement or claim as the task, so the author could sensibly build on it or refer back to it;
- `RELATED`: the same specific subject, entity or question, but the task makes a different point;
- `UNRELATED`: otherwise.

The prompt also states that the task and the earlier note both come from an account that writes about AI, so being about AI, or about the same broad field, is not a connection. This is the sentence E3A added after the same judge family failed its random-pair control on these 234 notes (deepseek-flash rated 70.9% of 230 random pairs 0 under the first scale and 81.7% under the second, against 90% required; the same-story level stayed clean, about 1% below cosine 0.4, while the broad level was liberal). `RELATED` is E3A's second-scale level 1. Both prompt texts (normal and reversed option order) are in `config.json` with their sha256.

A reply is accepted if, after removing whitespace, quotes, asterisks and a final full stop and upper-casing, it is exactly SAME, RELATED or UNRELATED; anything else (including "not related" or a sentence that contains a label) is a format failure and is retried once with the same request. The provider has no seed, so temperature 0 and the seed field do not make a call reproducible; the retry and the repeat measure run-to-run drift. An empty reply, an error or a refusal is recorded as a format failure with its class; a refusal by the spend cap stops the run. A call is one request through the backend, which itself retries transport errors and empty completions (up to four attempts in all); only the final outcome of a call counts toward the 2% rule, so the proxy may have served more requests than the records count. The proxies' `/arena/summary` (thinking disabled, no decoding overrides, no refused or unknown-usage calls) is read before and after each stage and stored. Pairs not in the pool are never judged and never counted as relevant (the usual pooling assumption; every method's own top-1 is in the pool by construction, and so is that of HYB_SHIPPED on all 214 tasks).

Strict relevance = `SAME`; lenient = `SAME` or `RELATED`. Strict is the only primary relevance. The verdict is issued under (a) strict relevance with the judge's labels and must be the same under (c) strict relevance with the assistant's labels on the decisive pairs (below), otherwise it is `UNRESOLVED (labels)` (the prevalence check is repeated on the merged labels; if it fails there, the assistant verdict is `NOT_EVALUABLE (prevalence)` and the verdict becomes `UNRESOLVED (labels)`); (b) lenient relevance is reported descriptively. Prevalence check: if fewer than 25 eligible tasks have at least one `SAME` note in their pool, P1 is not evaluable, the decision table is not applied and the lenient table is reported beside it. The number of tasks with a `SAME` note, the numbers of discordant tasks and the pairs without a valid label are printed next to every verdict.

Decisive pairs. At q = 0.20, for each contrast (EMB_MINILM against LEX_KERNEL, HYB_KERNEL against LEX_KERNEL), the tasks that are in exactly one of the two fire sets, or in both with different first candidates; the decisive pairs are the first candidates of the two methods on those tasks, over both contrasts (51 pairs on the draft data). They depend on scores only, so `prepare` exports them and the assistant labels them before the judge is called (below).

Judge gate (all must hold before any label is used for a U1 reading):
1. positive control (a format check): 60 pairs (brief `k`, note `k`) for `k` drawn from the eligible tasks (seeded): at least 55 `SAME` (a judge right 95% of the time passes with probability 0.92, one right 90% of the time with 0.44);
2. negative control: 60 pairs (brief `i`, note `j`, `j < i`), seeded, 30 random and 30 hard (MiniLM cosine of brief and note in [0.25, 0.40) and at most one shared content stem; about 2,700 such pairs on the draft data): at most 3 of the 60 labelled `SAME` (pass probability 0.97 at a true false-SAME rate of 2%, 0.65 at 5%, 0.28 at 8%). The share rated `UNRELATED` is reported per stratum next to E3A's rates and is not a gate;
3. perturbed repeat: a seeded 10% of the pooled pairs plus every decisive pair judged a second time with the three answer options listed in the reverse order: at least 80% of the first-pass `SAME` labels are `SAME` again; the share of identical three-way labels and Cohen's kappa for `SAME` versus not-`SAME` are reported (a repeat with the same prompt would measure only run-to-run drift);
4. decisive-pair audit: the assistant labels the decisive pairs before the judge is called, blind, in shuffled order without method names or scores, and the sha256 of its label file is recorded in Amendment 2. Gate: at least 75% agreement with the judge on `SAME` versus not-`SAME` over the decisive set (pass probability for 51 pairs 0.97 at a true agreement of 85%, 0.79 at 80%, 0.48 at 75%); a decisive pair with no valid judge label counts as a disagreement. The stability of the verdict under the assistant's labels is not part of the gate: it is read through the verdict (`UNRESOLVED (labels)`). Agreement is reported separately for pairs found only by lexical methods, only by MiniLM and by both, with Wilson 95% intervals (reported, not gated). A seeded sample of 30 other pooled pairs, 10 per judge label, is labelled the same way after the judge's labels exist and reported descriptively. These are not a human ground truth (the reader is a language model too); the owner may audit the files;
5. format failures at most 2% of calls; a pair with no valid label after the retry counts as not relevant, is listed, and if it is a decisive pair takes the assistant's label. The 2% is counted over calls and includes the failures the retry recovered from, whereas the audit of gate 4 counts a decisive pair without a valid judge label as a disagreement even where the assistant's label for it is not `SAME`.

The controls certify the format and the extremes, not the boundary between `SAME` and `RELATED` on first candidates (a rule such as "SAME if the cosine is at least 0.40" passes both); gate 4 audits that boundary. If the gate fails, the labels are not used for the U1 readings, the U1 tables are left out of `results.json` and `summary.json` (`gate_failed` true) and the report says so; U2, U3 and the implementation controls still stand.

Readings, for method `m` and sweep fraction `q` in {0.10, 0.20, 0.30}:
- Fire set `F_m(q)`: every method's score is computed for every earlier note of every task with the kernel gates removed (see Methods), so every task has a candidate and every method fires exactly `ceil(q * T)` tasks, 22, 43 and 65 at q = 0.10, 0.20, 0.30 (`T` = 214): the `ceil(q * T)` tasks with the highest score `s`, ties by task index. A task with no candidate is never fired; if fewer than `ceil(q * T)` tasks have a candidate, the method fires on all of them and the shortfall is reported (none on the draft data).
- `hits_m(q)`: tasks in `F_m(q)` whose first candidate is relevant. `false_m(q)` = `|F_m(q)| - hits_m(q)`, the false injections. Equal exposure makes hits and false injections directly comparable between methods.
- Label-free overlap of the fire sets (printed by `prepare` before any judge call; A = first method, B = LEX_KERNEL; discordant = only A + only B + shared tasks with different first candidates; decisive pairs = only A + only B + 2 x different-first; max |D| = max(only A, only B) + different-first; draft values, the frozen run's values replace them):

| contrast | q | shared | same first | only A | only B | discordant tasks | decisive pairs | max abs D |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| HYB_KERNEL vs LEX_KERNEL | 0.10 | 22 | 22 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| | 0.20 | 36 | 34 | 7 | 7 | 16 | 18 | 9 |
| | 0.30 | 54 | 46 | 11 | 11 | 30 | 38 | 19 |
| EMB_MINILM vs LEX_KERNEL | 0.10 | 11 | 8 | 11 | 11 | 25 | 28 | 14 |
| | 0.20 | 27 | 18 | 16 | 16 | 41 | 50 | 25 |
| | 0.30 | 45 | 26 | 20 | 20 | 59 | 78 | 39 |

- **P1 (primary)**: `D_emb = hits_EMB_MINILM(0.20) - hits_LEX_KERNEL(0.20)` under strict relevance. **P1b (fusion check)**: `D_hyb = hits_HYB_KERNEL(0.20) - hits_LEX_KERNEL(0.20)`. Each has a 95% interval from the paired bootstrap specified below (fire sets recomputed inside every resample). Margin `delta = 6` tasks (2.8% of the 214 eligible tasks, 14% of the 43 fired).
  - `EMBEDDER_HELPS_GATE`: lower bound of the `D_emb` interval above 0 and `D_emb >= delta`. This is a significance rule with a point-estimate margin, not proof that the gain reaches `delta`.
  - `LEXICAL_SUFFICIENT_FOR_GATE`: the upper bounds of both the `D_emb` and the `D_hyb` intervals below `delta`.
  - `UNRESOLVED` otherwise.
  - `EMB_MINILM` carries the verdict because with the calibration above `HYB_KERNEL` and `LEX_KERNEL` fire almost the same tasks (table above): `|D_hyb| <= 9`, and `D_hyb` is 0 by construction at q = 0.10. This was found from scores alone, before any judge call (Amendment 1). `D_hyb` tests whether the shipped max-fusion keeps what the embedder channel finds: if `EMBEDDER_HELPS_GATE` is issued and `D_hyb < D_emb / 2`, the recommendation is to add the embedder profile but not to ship the current max-fusion unchanged (fit the channel weights on the shadow data in a separate preregistered slice).
- **Bootstrap (every U1 interval).** Resample indices come from `bootstrap_indices(n, draws, seed)` (splitmix64, vendored into `scripts/wmb/stats.py` with a test pinning it to `scripts/analyze_writer_memory_sim.bootstrap_indices`, so the stream does not depend on the numpy or Python version), seed 20261102. A resample is a multiset of the 214 tasks (copies count separately; the cut at `ceil(q * size)` may split copies of one task; ties by task index); the fire sets are recomputed inside every resample; `tau_lex` and `tau_emb` stay at their full-sample values. `D_emb` and `D_hyb` use 100,000 draws, every other interval 10,000. The interval is [sorted(D*)[floor(0.025 * draws)], sorted(D*)[ceil(0.975 * draws) - 1]]. A day-block bootstrap (the 63 days of the eligible tasks, numbered in the order of their sorted date strings, which is chronological, resampled with the same seed and n = 63, a day's tasks kept together, the cut at `ceil(q * size)` with `size` the number of tasks in the resample) is computed beside it, and the interval used for the verdict is the wider of the two at each end. The primary verdict is recomputed with the stability seeds 20271102, 20271103, 20271104; if the four verdicts are not identical the verdict is `UNRESOLVED (resampling boundary)` and the four are listed.
- Secondary and exploratory readings are descriptive, with unadjusted 95% intervals, and cannot change or qualify the verdict; no other q, relevance or method pair counts toward the decision. A sweep of the kernel's evidence floor (`min_evidence` 0, 0.4, 0.8, 1.2) and a sweep of the channel weights are not preregistered readings of this slice: they are not computed and no default thresholds are proposed from them.
  - hits and false injections of every method at every q; `D_emb` and `D_hyb` at q = 0.30 and under lenient relevance (`D_hyb` at q = 0.10 is not read: the fire sets are identical and it is 0 by construction); `LEX_BM25`, `LEX_TFIDF` and `HYB_SHIPPED` against `LEX_KERNEL`; `hits@3` (fired tasks with a relevant note among the method's first three); `REF_E5` (the same 43 tasks as `EMB_MINILM` at q = 0.20: fire rate, hits, false injections);
  - `D_bar`: the mean of the hits difference at equal exposure over q in {0.10, 0.15, 0.20, 0.25, 0.30, 0.35, 0.40} for EMB_MINILM and HYB_KERNEL against LEX_KERNEL, same bootstrap, fire sets recomputed at every q;
  - operating point: for each method and q the precision of the first candidate; the largest q on a 0.02 grid at which the cumulative strict precision of the first candidates is at least 0.80 (E3A's bar) and its coverage; at the shipped thresholds (lex_tau 0.30, min_evidence 0.8, tau 0.57) the hits and false injections of the first candidates of the tasks the lexical profile fires and of those fired only through the embedding gate, with Wilson 95% intervals; the number of tasks with a `SAME` note in the pool.

## Part U3: questions with little word overlap

120 notes drawn by seed, 40 from each length tercile of all 234 notes. For each, flash (writer proxy) writes one question whose answer is a specific detail in the note and which must not reuse the note's distinctive words (paraphrase). Prompt in `config.json`. Gate: an answerability check by flash on the judge proxy ("does this note contain the answer?"): at least 95% yes; questions failing it are dropped and counted. P2 is issued only if this gate passes. Overlap of a question = share of its content stems found in the source note; terciles by overlap over the retained questions, the retained questions sorted by (overlap, note index), sizes floor(n/3), floor(n/3) and the rest; the lowest tercile is the first floor(n/3). `questions` runs once and is never regenerated.

Memory = all 234 notes (no time restriction; this is question answering about the past, not task-time recall). Gold = the source note. For each method: recall@1, @3, @10 and MRR, overall and by overlap tercile.

- **P2 (descriptive of size, not a test of existence)**: in the lowest-overlap tercile, `D3_emb = recall@3(EMB_MINILM) - recall@3(LEX_KERNEL)` (source note only), paired bootstrap over questions (10,000 draws, the generator and seed above); `D3_hyb = recall@3(HYB_KERNEL) - recall@3(LEX_KERNEL)` is reported beside it.
  - `EMBEDDER_RESCUES`: lower bound of the `D3_emb` interval above 0 and `D3_emb >= 0.15`; otherwise "not `EMBEDDER_RESCUES`". There is no `NO_RESCUE` verdict: with 40 questions its condition (upper bound under 0.10) is met only if the methods disagree on very few questions or the embedder is worse.
  - The lowest tercile is chosen on the quantity the lexical score uses and the questions are written to avoid the note's words, so a positive `D3_emb` is expected by construction: P2 measures how large the lexical loss on paraphrased questions is. It does not show that the persona phase needs an embedder; that is decided on U2 extended with distractors of its own kind (other biography-like events).
  - Another note may also answer a question. For every question in the lowest tercile and for `EMB_MINILM` and `LEX_KERNEL`, each of the first three candidates that is not the source note is checked with the answerability prompt (`audit` command, at most 240 calls); `recall_any@3` counts a question as recalled when the source note or a retrieved note that answers it is among the first three. Both are reported; P2 uses the source-only recall.

## Part U2: the persona's facts (smoke test, descriptive)

Index = the 26 biography items (origin `fiction_autobio`, dates as in the persona file) plus the 234 notes as distractors. Query = each of the 24 fact questions, task type `personal`. Gold = the biography items that contain any of the question's key strings (case-insensitive, whole word). Questions with an empty gold set after this rule are excluded and counted: f11 and f23 (their key appears only inside a longer word), so 22 of 24 are scored; f10 has four gold items, so its recall is easy. Methods: the five of U1 and `KERNEL_NAMES` (HYB_KERNEL with the name channel on; names are off everywhere else). recall@1 and @3 for each method, overall and by question style (`direct`, `indirect`). With 22 questions nothing here can decide anything; it is a check that the pipeline works on the persona and a first look at the name problem the kernel's name channel exists for.

## Implementation controls (free, run before any model call)

- C1 self-retrieval: for every eligible task `k`, the brief of note `k` against all 234 notes, non-causally. Gate: note `k` is among the first three candidates of every method for at least 90% of tasks (draft data: 0.958 to 0.991), and with each brief shifted by one note (the brief of note `k+1` against note `k`) note `k` is the first candidate for fewer than 10% of tasks (draft data: at most 0.005). The share with note `k` first is reported, not gated (draft data: 0.883 for EMB_MINILM, 0.902 for LEX_KERNEL, 0.916 for HYB_KERNEL and LEX_TFIDF, 0.930 for LEX_BM25): near-twin notes legitimately outrank the source.
- C2 causality: for every method and task, no candidate has an index at or above the task's; for 25 seeded tasks the task's kernel index holds exactly the notes `j < i` and its document frequencies equal those recomputed from the first `i` notes.
- C3 the kernel path agrees with an independent reference implementation of its coverage and cosine on a seeded 30 tasks: rank 1 identical, LEX_KERNEL coverage within 1e-8 and HYB_KERNEL strength within 5e-6 (the kernel rounds scores to four decimals and the hybrid score is 20 times the strength; draft data: 4.7e-11 and 2.4e-6).
- C4 decision chain: the analysis and an independent re-implementation (written from this text, without reading the analysis code) return identical `D_emb`, interval bounds and verdicts on 1,000 synthetic label sets with a planted `D_emb` of 0, 3, 6 and 9 tasks; `EMBEDDER_HELPS_GATE` is issued at most 5% of the time at 0 and its frequency rises with the planted gain. After the judge, a within-task shuffle of the labels is reported as a permutation reference with its analytic mean (the sum over tasks of the share of relevant pooled pairs times the difference of the two methods' first-candidate indicators), not as a pass/fail criterion.

## Decision table

| P1 (D_emb, with D_hyb) | P2 | what is recommended to the writing agent |
|---|---|---|
| EMBEDDER_HELPS_GATE | any | Add the embedder profile for note-writing memory. If D_hyb < D_emb / 2, do not ship HYB_KERNEL's current max-fusion unchanged: fit the channel weights on the shadow data in a separate preregistered slice. |
| LEXICAL_SUFFICIENT_FOR_GATE | not EMBEDDER_RESCUES | Stay lexical; no new dependency (for tasks of the brief kind). |
| LEXICAL_SUFFICIENT_FOR_GATE | EMBEDDER_RESCUES | Lexical for note-writing; an embedder is needed only for question-style recall (the persona and identity phase), to be decided then on U2 extended with distractors of its own kind. |
| UNRESOLVED (also UNRESOLVED (labels) and UNRESOLVED (resampling boundary)) | any | No new dependency now; run the kernel lexically in shadow. The question stays open: this analysis is repeated unchanged (frozen judge, code and prompts) on the notes published after the freeze once at least 100 exist (about 1,000 to 1,300 flash calls), and the embedder is not dropped from the roadmap before that. The shadow N1 record holds scores, ranks and keyed hashes but no texts or outcomes, so by itself it gives exposure rates, not hits. |

LEXICAL_SUFFICIENT_FOR_GATE and UNRESOLVED both mean no new dependency now; they differ in whether the question is closed. If the judge gate, the prevalence check or a control fails, the table is not applied and the report says which part is missing.

## Power, stated before the result

At q = 0.20, 43 tasks fire for every method. `HYB_KERNEL` and `LEX_KERNEL` share 36 of them (34 with the same first candidate), so only 16 tasks (18 pairs) can carry `D_hyb` and `|D_hyb| <= 9`; `EMB_MINILM` and `LEX_KERNEL` share 27 (18 with the same first candidate), so 41 tasks (50 pairs) can carry `D_emb` and `|D_emb| <= 25`. Simulated, with assumed label models (relevance a logistic function of a pair's cosine and coverage, calibrated to about 68 tasks with a SAME note in the pool; two independent simulations on the real score structure agree within a few points; task bootstrap only, the day-block interval is up to about 10% wider): the 95% interval for `D_emb` has a half-width of about 6.5 tasks, for `D_hyb` about 4.5. `EMBEDDER_HELPS_GATE` is returned in about 1% of runs at a true gain of 0, 3 to 8% at +3, 25 to 33% at +5 to +6, 70 to 76% at +9 and 80 to 97% at +10 to +12; a true gain just below `delta` is therefore called `EMBEDDER_HELPS_GATE` about one run in four. `LEXICAL_SUFFICIENT_FOR_GATE` needs the upper bound of `D_emb` below 6, which in practice means the lexical channel is at least as good: about 35% of runs at equal hits, about 80% when it is better by 5, under 10% for any true gain of +2 or more. `UNRESOLVED` is the most likely verdict for true gains between 0 and +6. With the calibrated weights `D_hyb` cannot show a gain of the size that matters: it reaches `EMBEDDER_HELPS_GATE` in about one run in three even when relevance follows the embedder's own cosine, against 81% for `D_emb`. P2 has 38 to 40 questions in its lowest tercile.

## Cost and safety

**U3** (119 of 120 questions retained, answerability 99.2%). Recall@3 overall: MiniLM 0.832, hybrid 0.824, BM25 0.815, kernel coverage 0.782, tf-idf 0.782 (recall@1: 0.681, 0.714, 0.706, 0.664, 0.714). Counting any retrieved note that also answers the question (`recall_any@3`, 179 audited pairs): MiniLM 0.795, kernel coverage 0.385.

**U2** (22 of 24 persona questions scored): recall@3 MiniLM 0.909, tf-idf 0.909, BM25 0.864, hybrid 0.682, kernel coverage 0.682. The name channel changed the rank of no question.

**The judge.** Its `SAME` is conservative and precise: against the assistant's blind labels, 45 of 51 decisive pairs and 28 of 30 stratified-sample pairs agree on `SAME` versus not (precision about 100% and 90%, recall about 75%). Its `RELATED` is liberal: 9 of the 10 sampled `RELATED` pairs were `UNRELATED` to the assistant. The six positive-control misses are systematic, not noise.

**What this means for the question asked.**
1. For note-writing tasks of the brief kind there is no sign that an embedder is needed: MiniLM 16 hits against BM25 17 and tf-idf 17 at equal exposure. This is exploratory; the preregistered verdict was not issued and the interval does not exclude a gain of up to 6 hits.
2. The kernel's own coverage score is the weakest lexical method in every part (U1 15 hits against 17; U3 recall@3 0.782 against 0.815, 0.359 against 0.436 in the lowest tercile; U2 recall@3 0.682 against 0.864). Replace it by BM25.
3. On paraphrased questions an embedder helps a lot (lowest tercile recall@3 +0.33 over kernel coverage and +0.26 over BM25, expected by construction), while on the persona's 22 facts tf-idf matches MiniLM.
4. The shipped max-fusion hybrid keeps little of the embedder's gain (lowest tercile 0.487 against 0.692): do not ship it unchanged; if an embedder is added, fuse by rank or fit the weights separately.

**Recommendation to the writing agent.** No new dependency now. Use BM25 in place of coverage (kernel 0.2). Revisit embeddings for question-style recall at the persona stage. Repeat the U1 analysis (R1b) with a revised judge protocol whose gate is passed on fresh controls, and on at least 100 notes published after the freeze.
