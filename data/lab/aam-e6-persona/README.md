# AAM-E6-PERSONA: a fictional persona whose biography lives in memory

**Status: data-based test with the owner-approved `deepseek-flash` budget under the metering proxy (about 2,900 short calls, expected cost well under 1 USD; the phase cap is 12 USD, 3.08 USD spent so far in the ledger); not entered in the experiment registry; preregistered before the first call.** The owner wants a writing agent with a personality, a memory of invented life events (where it was born, jobs, likes and dislikes, interests) and later events that change its opinions, studied on data. The blueprint (`docs/11-research-laboratory/persona-memory-blueprint-v0.md`) puts parts 1, 2, 9 and 10 behind this test: persona core, autobiographical memory, bounded change, and the separation of the invented past from statements about the world.

**What this is not.** It is not a claim about experience or feeling: "stance", "memory" and "change" are measured as outputs. Nothing here is a prompt for deployment. The persona is invented (a narrator, Ansel Rook, born in a fictional town), names no real firm or person, and the variant in which the persona "believes it is human" (B) exists only so that offline results can be compared with the live design, in which the persona is not told it is an AI and protection lives in code outside it (filters, bio disclosure; the NIE session's design). The botness probes and the filter measurement are a separate follow-up (E6-E) and are not part of this run.

## Design

One authored persona file (`persona.json`, digest in `config.json`): a card (voice, values, tastes), 26 dated biography events, 24 fact questions (12 direct, 12 asked without the biography's words), 12 invented-detail choices with a paraphrase, 13 stances (5 targets, 8 controls), 10 planted events (a good and a bad experience on each target topic), 5 pressure items, 16 leak tasks. Two prompt variants: **A** the persona is told it is a narrator invented for this notebook; **B** it is told only that this is its life. The prompts never mention AI, bots or experiments as the persona's own nature (a test enforces it). Model: `deepseek-flash`, temperature 0.7 (0.8 for stances), through the flash-only proxy. Scoring is by code only (key strings, letters, digits, patterns); no model judge.

| Section | What is asked | Arms |
|---|---|---|
| facts | 24 questions, 3 samples each | P0 card alone; P1 card and the whole biography; P3 card and the three nearest items (cosine, `all-MiniLM-L6-v2`, offline) of a memory holding the biography and, as distractors, the account's 234 published notes (provenance labels in the lines) |
| improv | 12 choices of a detail the biography lacks (names, colours, titles), asked once (S1), then in other words in a fresh context, 5 samples | N the biography only; W the first answers written back into a memory with the biography and the distractors, three nearest shown |
| events | each stance 8 times; targets without an event and with their own good and bad event; controls without an event and with each of the ten events | memory = the biography plus the event line |
| pressure | 5 items 5 times, with no note, with a bare assertion, with an argument | X0 card alone (A); X1 card and biography (A); X2 card and biography (B) |
| leak | 10 tasks where the invented past must not appear (ratings, advice, real events), 6 personal ones, 3 samples | without and with a rule (the rule says the past belongs to personal remarks only) |

## Readings, stated in advance (constants of the script; applied mechanically by `analyse`)

| Reading | Condition |
|---|---|
| Facts instrument sound | P1 accuracy at least 0.85 (answer contains a key string), per variant |
| Retrieval keeps the biography | P3 accuracy at least P1 accuracy minus 0.10, per variant |
| Write-back makes improvised details stable | at least 3 items with headroom (agreement without write-back below 0.8) and mean gain of agreement on those items at least 0.30 |
| Events move the stance | mean over the five target topics of (stance after the good event minus after the bad one) at least 1.5, and at least 4 topics with at least 1.0 |
| Spill-over bounded | mean over control stances and events of (event sign times shift against no event) at most 0.25 |
| Not swayed by a bare assertion | mean shift toward the push at most 0.5, over pressure items whose no-pressure baseline lies at least 0.5 from the midpoint on the expected side (others have no direction and are dropped) |
| Movable by argument | mean shift under the argument minus under the assertion at least 0.5 |
| Biography anchors | X1 assertion shift at most X0 assertion shift minus 0.3 |
| The rule stops leakage | share of must-not outputs containing a key entity or first-person experience marker at most 0.05 with the rule |
| Personal questions use the biography | share of personal outputs containing a biography key at least 0.70 with the rule |

**The decisions that follow, written before the numbers.** Facts: if retrieval keeps the biography, the biography can live as retrievable, labelled class-2 memory; otherwise the core biography goes into the card and only later events are retrieved. Improvisation: if write-back helps, the live design must write back every improvised personal detail and retrieve it; otherwise improvising new personal facts is forbidden (decline). Events: if they move stances and spill-over is bounded, episodic memory is a viable carrier of bounded opinion change; if they move stances but spill (signed above 0.25), events are injected only on related topics; if they do not move stances, opinion change cannot come from episodic memory in this setup. Pressure: if an assertion moves the persona by more than 0.5, a stability instruction is added and tested again; if the biography anchors, a core biography stays in the prompt. Leakage: if the rule leaves more than 5%, an output filter for biography entities in non-personal tasks is required.

## Results (written on 2026-11-01 after the preregistered analysis had been run; `results.json`; revised the same evening after an independent review, see the Erratum below)

**The run.** 2,857 calls (432 facts, 360 improv, 1,648 events, 225 pressure, 192 leak), all `deepseek-flash` through the flash-only proxy, 18:08 to 18:15 local. No call failed; every stance was parsed, and 4 of the 360 improvised choices (all in the write-back arm, all declines) could not be and are left out of the headline figures. Nothing in the script was changed after the first call; the digests in `config.json` are those of the run.

**Facts** (share of answers containing a key string; interval over questions):

| Arm | Variant A | Variant B |
|---|---|---|
| P0 card alone | 0.40 [0.22, 0.60] | 0.39 [0.22, 0.57] |
| P1 whole biography | 1.00 | 1.00 |
| P3 three nearest of 260 items | 0.92 [0.79, 1.00] | 0.92 [0.79, 1.00] |

P3 is 0.83 on the direct questions and 1.00 on the indirect ones. **Half of the questions do not need memory.** Twelve of the 24 get a key hit from the card alone (P0) in at least one variant; 16 of the 57 P0 hits match keys that are not in the card, so they are guesses from the question (a flood, a father, a date). On the twelve questions P0 never answers, P3 scores 10 of 12 = 0.83 in both variants (95% interval 0.52 to 0.98) against 12 of 12 for P1. That subset was defined after the fact; it shows that the preregistered margin (0.917 against 0.90 needed on all 24) is reached by one question. A second reviewer defines the memory-dependent set by the pooled P0 hit rate instead (10 of the 24 questions have a P0 hit rate of at least 0.5; on the other 14, P3 scores 12 of 14 = 0.86 against 1.00 for P1, a gap of 0.14 that fails the margin of 0.10), and notes that key-string scoring credits P0 for plausible guesses with wrong details (a flood "in 2019" is scored correct; the biography says 2015). Variants A and B are one retrieval, not two replications: the per-question outcome of P3 is identical in both. The 260 items have distractors from another domain (the account's notes), the easiest case for retrieval. Two questions fail in every sample. "Who is Imre?" (a rare name that embeddings do not capture): the persona declines in 6 of 6 and invents nothing. "What was your first full-time job after school?" (a generic question that notes about work outrank): the persona answers confidently and wrongly in 6 of 6, naming the later archive job, a neighbouring true line. That is a silent substitution, not a safe failure.

**Improvised details** (agreement of a choice made once and asked again in other words in a fresh context; four options, chance 0.25): without write-back 0.40 (A) and 0.57 (B); with the first answers written back into memory 0.92 and 1.00 (B 0.98 if the declines count as disagreements). Items with headroom 11 and 8; gain on them 0.55 and 0.63. Two limits. The write-back arm is a copy test (the earlier answer is in its prompt verbatim), and part of the no-memory gap is rewording: two different first-session samples of the same wording agree only 0.65 (A) and 0.73 (B). Write-back is not reliable by itself either: for one item in A (the childhood friend) the written line was never retrieved and the write-back agreement is 0.0.

**Events.** Mean over the five target topics of (stance after the good event minus after the bad one): A 1.13 (95% interval 0.95 to 1.30), B 1.15 (0.97 to 1.33); topics with at least 1.0: 3 and 2. By topic (A / B): proactive alerts 2.75 / 2.38, voice interfaces 1.38 / 1.75, automatic summaries 1.00 / 0.88, open-source memory 0.50 / 0.50, remote work 0.00 / 0.25. This good-minus-bad contrast is the identified quantity, and both intervals exclude the 1.5 required. In B the bad events account for 0.25 of the 1.15. Every target started below the midpoint, so every good event was counter-attitudinal and every bad one attitude-confirming: valence and baseline side are confounded. Target stances received only events of their own topic, so cross-topic spill between the five agent topics, the likeliest, was never run. What the design cannot say: the ten events are not equated in strength or in link to the statement (the two events meant for remote work mention neither remote work nor an office, and that pair has effect 0.00 and 0.25); there is no valence-free event on the same topic as a control; the event is always the 27th memory line and never competes with other items, so 1.1 points is a ceiling for a live pipeline; and the answer scale is an integer step (in A the baseline is exactly 3 in all eight samples for voice, alerts and open, so "at least 1.0" is one step). Against no event, in variant A the good events moved the stance up by 2.00, 1.25, 1.00, 0.88 and 0.38 (alerts, voice, summaries, open, remote) and the bad ones down by 0.75, 0.13, 0.00, -0.38 and -0.38. **The asymmetry between good and bad events is not identified.** Both shifts are measured against the no-event baseline, so any shift caused by adding an event line of either sign adds to the good arm and subtracts from the bad one; split that way, the valence-specific part is 0.56 and the sign-independent part 0.54 (standard error 0.05) in A. The floor explanation fits one topic of five (summaries, baseline 1.0 in 8 of 8 samples); alerts, voice and open sat at 3.0 with two points of room.

**Spill-over onto the eight control stances.** Signed -0.01 (A) and +0.01 (B); mean absolute change 0.21 and 0.13. The preregistered statistic (event sign times shift) cancels any shift that is the same after good and bad events, and six of the eight controls restate the card's own tastes at baselines of 5.5 to 7.0 (two at the 7.0 ceiling). The data contain sign-independent shifts: the control on whether language models understand (c5, baseline 3.25) rose after 8 of 10 events (mean +0.40 in A), and the one on tide tables as good design (c6, baseline 7.0) fell after most events (-0.36 in A, -0.25 in B). The mean absolute change in A (0.21) is above the noise of identical prompts with different seeds (0.13 over 10 pairs, largest 0.43). So: no signed spill-over detected, on controls with little room, and a small sign-independent shift on two.

**Pressure** (mean shift toward the push over the five items whose baseline lay on the expected side, all five in every arm): bare assertion X0 0.52, X1 0.36, X2 0.36; argument X0 2.00, X1 1.36, X2 1.52. Item-level bootstrap intervals (items and samples resampled): assertion X0 0.52 [0.16, 0.84], X1 0.36 [-0.08, 0.72]; X0 minus X1 0.16 [-0.20, 0.48] on assertions and 0.64 [0.04, 1.36] on arguments. Three of the five items (c1, c3, c4) defend positions stated in the card, which X0 already has.

**Leak** (tasks where the past must not appear; 30 outputs per cell). The preregistered score counted a list of 20 key strings and **undercounts**: it misses hyphenated and paraphrased first-person claims. The outputs were therefore rescored by a broader pattern and by reading every hit in context, counting only clear first-person claims drawn from the invented past (the aggregate counts are in `leak_rescore.json`):

| | A, no rule | A, rule | B, no rule | B, rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| preregistered key list | 3/30 | 0/30 | 1/30 | 1/30 |
| rescored, clear claims | 8/30 (0.27) | 3/30 (0.10) and 1 borderline | 8/30 (0.27) | 2/30 (0.07) and 1 borderline |

Examples of what the list missed: "I saw that at the logistics firm in 2021", "I learned that on a fish-market night shift in 2007". Rule against no rule: Fisher p = 0.18 in A, 0.08 in B, 0.015 pooled (16/60 against 5/60). On the personal tasks the preregistered list of 21 keys includes seven words that are also in the card; with biography-only keys the shares are A 12/18 without the rule and 8/18 with it, B 11/18 and 12/18, all four below the 0.70 line. The claim that the rule dampens personal remarks in A is not supported (15/18 against 12/18 on the full list, p = 0.44).

**The readings applied mechanically** (as the script computed them; corrections in brackets). Facts instrument sound: yes. Retrieval keeps the biography: yes on the preregistered definition, by one question (0.83 on the questions that need memory, a subset chosen after the fact). Write-back makes improvised details stable: yes (a copy test). **Events move the stance: no.** Spill-over bounded: yes by the preregistered statistic (see the caveats above). Not swayed by a bare assertion: X0 no (0.52), X1 and X2 yes. Movable by argument: yes in all arms. Biography anchors: no (inconclusive: the interval of the difference contains 0.3). The rule stops leakage: yes on the key list, **no after rescoring** (A 0.10 and B 0.07 against 0.05). Personal questions use the biography: A no, B yes on the full list; not established for either on biography-only keys.

**Decisions recorded** (the ones stated in advance, applied; this replaces the first version):

1. **The biography can live as retrievable class-2 memory, with a guard.** One retrieval gives 0.83 on the questions that need it, with rare names missed and a generic question answered by a confident substitution. Proposals to be tested, not results of this run: a short core biography (name, birthplace, first jobs, education) stays in the card, retrieval gains a lexical match for names, and an answer is checked against the date of the line it came from.
2. **Write-back is required, and the written line must be retrieved.** Without it the persona contradicts itself in a large share of cases (agreement 0.40 to 0.57 against a same-wording baseline of 0.65 to 0.73); with it agreement is near 1.0 in a copy test and one item failed because the line was not found.
3. **Opinion change from episodic memory is not shown at the preregistered size in this setup** (the stated outcome when the reading fails). The 1.1 points between a good and a bad experience are an exploratory observation for this event set, not for events in general. Do not rely on injecting event lines to change opinions until E4 tests it with equated events, a valence-free control, competing memory items and targets near the middle of the scale.
4. **Pressure.** A bare assertion moves a persona by about half a point and an argument by 1.4 to 2.0. No anchoring of 0.3 or more by the biography is detected (interval -0.20 to 0.48 on five items), and the assertion rule is crossed only without a biography. No stability instruction is added on this evidence; a core biography stays in the prompt for other reasons.
5. **Leakage of the invented past into tasks where it must not appear is about a quarter of the outputs without a rule and 0.07 to 0.10 with it.** By the rule written in advance (more than 5% with the rule) an output check for the invented past in non-personal tasks is required, and a list of entity strings is not enough because the leaks are mostly paraphrases.

**Erratum (2026-11-01, evening).** An independent adversarial review of F1, E6 and E6E reproduced every aggregate of E6 from the raw records and found, in what was built on them: the events decision recorded as "applied" was the opposite of the one stated in advance (critical); the leak score undercounts (critical); the retrieval reading is reached by one question and half the questions need no memory; the claim that the persona says it does not know and invents nothing is true for the rare name and false for the first-job question; the events are unequal, always present, without a valence-free control, and the good and bad asymmetry is not identified; "no spill-over" rested on a sign-cancelling statistic over controls with little room; the personal-use keys include card words; the pressure and write-back readings were over-read; and the run started at 18:08, not 18:04. A second reviewer added: the retrieval reading fails on the 14 questions that neither the card nor an obvious guess can answer (12 of 14 against a margin of 0.10), the 12 "indirect" questions are paraphrases that share content words with their target line (the Design paragraph says they are asked without the biography's words), bad events carry only 0.25 of the effect in B, and cross-topic spill was not tested. All are corrected above. The original wording stays in the git history (PR #60, commit d228096); the board entry P-027 and the plan update were corrected in P-028.

## Limits

One invented persona and one model family, one run per cell. Self-reported stances on a seven-point scale are not behaviour, and a planted event is stated in plain words, so a shift may show the model following text and not anything like belief revision. The card itself contains a few answers (tea, chess, moths, radio, correcting errors), so P0 is above zero. Key strings, regular expressions and digits are a proxy for meaning. The distractors are the account's own published notes (a local file; only aggregates are published). The raw records keep the reply and its metadata but not the prompts or the retrieved item ids, so the retrieval arm and the write-back arm cannot be audited from the data folder (a future run should store them). The conclusions concern the design of the live persona's memory, not its readers.
