## Bibliographic check (authors, year, venue, volume and pages: match / differ, and how)

- Authors match: Peter Haehner, Michael D. Krämer, Amanda J. Wright, Wiebke Bleidorn. The digest's "Kramer" should be "Krämer".
- Year, venue, volume and pages: online 3 July 2025; print issue March 2026, *European Journal of Personality* 40(2):391-412. The digest's 2025 matches the online-first year; if volume and pages are cited, the issue year is 2026.
- Claim scope matches: seven panel studies and N = 196,256 (Table 2, p. 394).

## What the source says about the claim, with a locator (page, section, table or figure)

- Abstract, p. 391 (current corrected PDF): "event-related changes in the Big Five personality traits were small (average bstd = 0.06)".
- Corrigendum, Abstract correction (online 19 March 2026): the original "(average bstd = 0.05) should read (average bstd = 0.06)". The correction follows a mis-specified `D>+2Y` dummy in the data-preparation scripts. The corrigendum reports that three specific conclusions changed, beginning with more consistent evidence for long-term effects; it continues to report very small average effect sizes.
- Results, p. 404: "The average absolute standardized effect size of significant effects across events, outcomes, and the different event-related dummies was bstd = 0.08, ranging from bstd = 0.06 for personality traits and bstd = 0.07 for self-esteem to bstd = 0.17 for life satisfaction." This 0.08 is not the abstract's average Big Five change: it averages significant effects across all outcomes and dummies, including life satisfaction.
- OSF project node `v8csw`, description (API record modified 30 October 2026): "event-related changes in the Big Five personality traits were small (standardized bMean = 0.08)". This is stale project-page text. The v1 preprint abstract (29 November 2024, DOI `https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/jcbu4`) also said 0.08; the v2 preprint abstract (16 September 2025) says 0.06.
- Resolution: 0.05 is the original online published abstract value, corrected to 0.06. 0.08 is the stale OSF project description and an older preprint summary, and the same numeral appears in the results as a different statistic. It is not a second live estimate of the abstract's average Big Five effect.

## Verdict: partly supported

The substantive claim (small average event-related Big Five changes across seven panels, N = 196,256) is supported. The digest's "0.05 vs 0.08 unresolved" framing is outdated: the authoritative corrected abstract value is 0.06, and 0.08 is stale OSF text plus a different results statistic.

## Scope limits the digest does not carry (population, method, delay, effect size)

- Coordinated analysis of seven existing panels (HILDA, HRS, LISS, MIDUS, NLSY, PAIRFAM, SOEP), not a new representative sample. The panels cover Australia, the United States, the Netherlands and Germany, with different assessment intervals (mostly 1-2 years; MIDUS 8-9 years).
- Fixed-effects models with control groups; event-related changes are estimated up to two years before and after the event and more than two years after it. The coefficients are within-person standardised associations, not causal effects.
- The average is across event-trait combinations. Effects are heterogeneous: only 19% of event-related dummy effects were significant, and event-related changes explained on average 0.04% of variance in Big Five traits (0.06% for self-esteem, 0.15% for life satisfaction).
- The coordinated analysis partly overlaps the Bühler et al. (2024) meta-analysis (about 12% of participants across events), so the two digest citations are not fully independent evidence.

## Suggested corrected wording for the digest

"Haehner, Krämer, Wright and Bleidorn (2025; print issue 2026), *European Journal of Personality* 40(2):391-412, coordinated analysis of 7 panels (N = 196,256), found small average standardised Big Five changes (average bstd = 0.06 in the 19 March 2026 corrected version; the original online abstract said 0.05)."
