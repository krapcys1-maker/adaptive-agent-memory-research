## Bibliographic check (authors, year, venue, volume and pages: match / differ, and how)

Match. The six authors, 2009, *Psychological Science*, volume 20, issue 7, pages 912-919 are confirmed by Crossref. The digest gives only "Frazier et al. 2009", which correctly identifies this paper; it does not carry the full author list or venue.

## What the source says about the claim, with a locator (page, section, table or figure)

- Abstract, p. 912: undergraduate students completed measures in typical posttraumatic-growth (PTG) domains at Time 1 and Time 2, two months apart. For the 122 participants who reported a traumatic event between the two times, the authors compared change in those measures with Posttraumatic Growth Inventory (PTGI) scores.
- Abstract, p. 912: "PTGI scores generally were unrelated to actual growth in PTG-related domains." Perceived growth was associated with increased distress from pre- to posttrauma, whereas actual growth was related to decreased distress; perceived, but not actual, growth was related to positive reinterpretation coping.
- Abstract, p. 912: the authors conclude that the PTGI, "and perhaps other retrospective measures", does not appear to measure actual pre- to posttrauma change.

## Verdict: supported

The paper supports the general claim that perceived and actual change can diverge. The digest's use is an analogy to stance change, however, not a direct test of it.

## Scope limits the digest does not carry (population, method, delay, effect size)

- Population and setting: undergraduate students; only the 122 participants who reported a traumatic event between Time 1 and Time 2 entered the focal analysis.
- Domain: posttraumatic growth after trauma, not the digest's 40 stance topics or ordinary value/belief change.
- Method and delay: self-report measures at two times two months apart; PTGI is a retrospective self-report measure, not an observer or behavioural measure.
- The study tests whether a retrospective growth inventory tracks measured pre-post change. It does not show that people are generally poor at reporting stance change over the digest's 90-365 day delays.
- Effect size is not carried in the abstract; the result is framed as PTGI scores being generally unrelated to actual growth.

## Suggested corrected wording for the digest

"perceived and actual change can diverge [B Frazier et al. 2009, posttraumatic growth: retrospective PTGI scores were generally unrelated to measured pre-post change over two months in undergraduates; use as an analogue only]".
