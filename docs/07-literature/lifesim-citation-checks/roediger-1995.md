## What the source says about the claim, with a locator (page, section, table or figure)

- Experiment 1 used six 12-word lists, each built from associates of a critical nonpresented word; 36 Rice University undergraduates heard and recalled the lists, then took a recognition test (p. 804).
- In Experiment 1, the critical lure was falsely recalled for 40% of the lists (p. 806). On recognition, the hit rate for studied items was .86, the false-alarm rate for unrelated lures was .02, and the false-alarm rate for critical lures was .84, approaching the hit rate (Table 1, p. 806).
- Experiment 2 used 24 fifteen-word lists. The critical lure was falsely recalled for 55% of the lists (p. 809). For critical lures, the false-alarm rate for critical lures was practically identical to the hit rate, .81 against .79 after study plus recall (Table 2, p. 809); the contributor also gives .72 for study plus arithmetic against .65, which the maintainers could not confirm. Page locators in this note are the contributor's and were not re-checked.
- The abstract (p. 803) summarises the finding as "remarkable levels of false recall and false recognition" in a list-learning paradigm, with false alarms to critical lures at a rate comparable to the hit rate in Experiment 2.

## Verdict: supported

The paper supports the empirical phenomenon the digest uses: elevated false alarms to critical lures, scored against unrelated new items and the studied-item hit rate. The digest's "thresholded gist traces" mechanism is explicitly a prediction, not a result claimed by Roediger and McDermott; the citation is attached to the human paradigm, which is correct.

## Scope limits the digest does not carry (population, method, delay, effect size)

- Laboratory list-learning with young undergraduate students; immediate free recall and recognition, not naturalistic episodes or long delays.
- The lists are built from strong associative norms around one critical word; the effect depends on that list structure and does not establish that gist traces are thresholded or that the same mechanism applies to LifeSim streams.
- Experiment 1 recognition followed prior recall of all six lists, so those recognition results are likely affected by prior recall; Experiment 2 varied this.
- The result is a high false-alarm rate to critical lures, not a demonstration that a particular gist-threshold model produces it.
- The digest's FM1 design spreads studied items over three or more days and lines rather than one list; that is an extrapolation beyond the original paradigm.

## Suggested corrected wording for the digest

Thresholded gist traces are predicted to raise lure false alarms (human DRM analogue: Roediger and McDermott 1995, list-learning paradigm) [B]."
