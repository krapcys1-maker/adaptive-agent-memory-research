# Citation check: Bühler et al. (2024)

## Citation as the digest gives it

"Buhler, Orth, Bleidorn et al. meta-analysis (44 studies, 121,187 participants; year 2023 or 2024 by source)."

## Where the digest uses it (search string, one sentence)

Search string: `Buhler, Orth, Bleidorn et al. meta-analysis` in `docs/11-research-laboratory/lifesim-design-panel-digest-2026-11-01.md` (line 489). ## Primary source found (DOI or stable URL; the version you read: preprint, accepted, published)

- Published article: Janina Larissa Bühler, Ulrich Orth, Wiebke Bleidorn, Elisa Weber, André Kretzschmar, Louisa Scheling and Christopher J. Hopwood, "Life Events and Personality Change: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis", *European Journal of Personality*, DOI `https://doi.org/10.1177/08902070231190219`. Read the published PDF (Vol. 38(3), pp. 544-568, 2024 issue; first published online 23 July 2023).
- OSF project with the effect sizes: `https://osf.io/dnjs2/`.

## Bibliographic check (authors, year, venue, volume and pages: match / differ, and how)

- The surname is Bühler, not Buhler; the digest's "Buhler" should be "Bühler".
- The published version is 2024, Vol. 38(3), pp. 544-568 (copyright 2023; first published online 23 July 2023). The digest's "2023 or 2024" is resolved: cite 2024 for the issue, with 2023 as the online-first year.
- Authors, title, venue, volume, pages and DOI match the published article.
- The sample counts match: 44 studies, 89 samples, 519 effect sizes and 121,187 participants (abstract p. 544; Results p. 551).

## What the source says about the claim, with a locator (page, section, table or figure)

- Abstract, p. 544: "In this preregistered meta-analysis, we summarize the available evidence on personality change in response to life events using data from 44 studies, including 89 samples with a total of 121,187 participants. Results supported reliable and specific albeit relatively small effects of life events on personality change. Effects were larger and more consistent in the work than in the love domain..."
- Results, p. 551: "The meta-analytic data set included 44 studies, which provided information on 89 samples and 519 effect sizes"; the average lag between pre- and post-event assessments was M = 2.45 years (SD = 2.44, range = 0.08-15.00); 121,187 participants were included.
- Results, p. 555: "analytically aggregated effect sizes were relatively small, with absolute values ranging between d = 0.000 and d = 0.337." Entering a new relationship, marriage and divorce showed significant effects for the largest number of personality constructs; there were no significant construct-specific effects for widowhood.
- The digest's "larger for work than for love" is directly supported (abstract p. 544). The authors describe the effects as reliable and specific but relatively small; "preliminary" is not their summary term.

## Verdict: supported

The published abstract contains both "44 studies" and "121,187 participants"; the issue year is 2024 (online first 2023). Minor corrections: spell the surname "Bühler", and do not attribute the word "preliminary" to this meta-analysis; its authors describe the effects as reliable and specific but relatively small.

## Scope limits the digest does not carry (population, method, delay, effect size)

- 44 studies, 89 samples and 519 effect sizes, with 121,187 participants; publication years 1995-2022.
- Almost all samples came from Western countries (96%), 94% were predominantly White/European, and there were no South American or African samples. Mean age was 38.15 years; 60% of samples were nationally representative; all measures were self-report; no sample was an intervention or prevention study.
- The meta-analysis combines pre-post standardised mean change (dE) with a comparison-group version (dEC). The average lag between pre- and post-event assessments was 2.45 years (range 0.08-15.00).
- Effects were small (absolute d from 0.000 to 0.337) and heterogeneous. Time lag significantly moderated love-domain effects but not work-domain effects. The design is observational, not causal.
- The data overlap with the Haehner et al. coordinated analysis (about 12% of participants across events), so the two digest citations are not fully independent confirmations.

## Suggested corrected wording for the digest

"Bühler, Orth, Bleidorn et al. (2024; first published online 2023), *European Journal of Personality* 38(3):544-568, a preregistered meta-analysis of 44 studies (89 samples, 519 effect sizes; N = 121,187), found reliable but relatively small average personality changes, with larger and more consistent effects in the work than in the love domain."
