## Research question, system, and method

Four direct replications (`n=16` each; total `n=64`) tested the reported vulnerability of an old finger-tapping sequence after reminder plus new-sequence learning. Three conceptual replications (`n=16` each; total `n=48`) used declarative digit/letter sequences similar to phone numbers/passwords, including a no-reminder group. Data, materials, and analysis scripts were posted to OSF.

## Extracted results

- Across four direct replications, accuracy changed by less than 1% on average rather than the roughly 57% decrement estimated from the original report; no experiment produced the predicted significant negative reconsolidation score.
- Directional meta-analytic Bayes factors favored a zero score over impairment for accuracy (`BF01=5.743`) and speed (`BF01=36.027`). Including the estimated original result still favored the null (`2.080` and `31.317`).
- In the conceptual studies, reminder-group sequence similarity fell from `1.0` at training to `0.750` at reminder and `0.638` at test. The no-reminder group also fell from `1.0` to `0.488`.
- Test recall was numerically better with reminder (`0.638`) than without (`0.488`); the preregistered-direction one-tailed comparison was nonsignificant in the opposite direction (`t(26.59)=-1.409`, `p=0.915`).

## Limitations and challenge pass

- Each individual experiment had only 16 participants; inference is strengthened by repeated nulls and pooled Bayesian analysis, not one large randomized trial.
- The work targets behavioral updating of procedural/declarative sequence memory. It does not test invasive pharmacological blockade or conditioned emotional responding.
- Conceptual tasks deliberately changed domain and encoding strength, so they test generality rather than directly replicating the motor finding.
- Retrieval-protection in the declarative tasks was numerical, not a significant benefit under the specified comparison.
- Prediction error was not cleanly manipulated, leaving proposed destabilization boundary conditions unresolved rather than disproven.

## Project relevance and falsifiable hypothesis

Retrieval can strengthen access rather than open destructive updating. Treat every read as non-mutating; require a separate, evidence-bearing revision transaction. Test simple retrieval, verified contradiction, unverified conflict, and no-retrieval update separately. Reject any automatic read-modify-write rule that changes durable beliefs after mere access or cannot distinguish storage loss from retrieval competition.
