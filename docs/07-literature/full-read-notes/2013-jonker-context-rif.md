## Method and result

The paper proposes that retrieval-induced forgetting can arise when study-to-practice processing changes internal context and the practice context is reinstated at final test. Three experiments manipulated these conditions and reported patterns predicted by the context account but not the authors' formulation of item inhibition. The review also documents cases where context reinstatement reduces directed-forgetting effects.

Exact locators: pp. 852–855 for the competing accounts and two tenets; experiments and results pp. 857–864; General Discussion pp. 864–870.

## Limitations

- This is a theory paper with supporting experiments, not a final adjudication of all RIF findings.
- Other studies report cue-independent and competition-dependent effects consistent with inhibition.
- Internal context is operationalized through task manipulations and is not one directly observed state variable.

## Project consequence

Never equate a behavioral interference curve with one hidden mechanism. Cross cue, context, version scope, and retrieval practice so that competition, context mismatch, and storage loss make different predictions.
