## Research question, population, and method

Two human experiments tested whether later recognition/recollection depends on reward contingency with semantic identity versus peripheral color, reward magnitude, outcome uncertainty, and contextual reward availability. Experiment 1 had 24 participants split into two groups of 12; Experiment 2 had 31 split into 15 and 16.

## Extracted results

- Experiment 1 had no overall main effect of reward on memory (`F(2,44)=0.27`, `p=0.74`), but a memory-condition × reward × group interaction (`F(2,44)=6.6`, `p<0.05`). Reward increased remember rates in the semantic-category group (`F(2,22)=3.9`, `p<0.05`) but not the color group (`F(2,22)=2.2`, `p=0.15`; Results, article pp. 3–4; Figure 2/Table 2).
- Doubling reward from €0.20 to €0.40 did not further increase recollection; the authors caution that the narrow range cannot establish a generally nonlinear law (Discussion, p. 5).
- Experiment 2's omnibus interactions were only trend-level (`p=0.095` corrected hits; `p=0.055` remember rates). Post-hoc recall was higher for rewarded items (`t(14)=2.22`, `p<0.05`) but uncertainty alone had no effect (`t(15)=-0.6`, `p=0.58`; p. 4).
- Reaction-time data for the uncertainty group survived for only four participants because of technical loss; those RT results are not reliable population evidence (p. 4).

## Limitations and challenge pass

- Small between-group samples, post-hoc tests after trend-level interactions, narrow monetary values, and technical data loss limit strength.
- Reward improved memory only when task-relevant semantic identity predicted it; generic reward/arousal did not uniformly boost all content.
- The experiment measures monetary contingency, not emotion broadly, and does not establish dopamine causally.

## Project relevance and falsifiable hypothesis

Consequences should attach to the memory feature needed for future decisions, not globally inflate every event in a rewarding session. Compare feature-contingent consequence labels with session-wide salience. Reject global reward propagation if it raises storage/retrieval of irrelevant co-occurring details without critical-recall benefit.
