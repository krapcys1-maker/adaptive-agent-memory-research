# Ego-Stengel and Wilson — Disruption of Ripple-Associated Hippocampal Activity During Rest Impairs Spatial Learning in the Rat

## Research question, system, and method

Six male Long-Evans rats were implanted for CA1 recording and ventral hippocampal commissure stimulation; one non-exploring animal was excluded. Five rats learned two visually similar spatial-navigation mazes for 8–10 days. Each 15-minute maze session was followed by one hour of rest. Online ripple detection triggered bilateral-afferent electrical stimulation only after the designated test maze, allowing within-animal comparison with a control maze. Thirty-eight sessions were completed; paired LFP analysis was possible for 33.

## Extracted results

- Rats completed fewer trajectories and traveled farther per trial in the maze whose post-training rest contained ripple-triggered stimulation (Figure 1B; Day × Maze `p<0.009` for completed trials; Maze `p<0.025` and interaction `p<10^-5` for distance).
- When only the first maze of each day was retained to control for possible carryover, Day × Maze remained significant (`p<0.05`; Results, Spatial learning).
- Choice errors were higher on the affected days (`p<0.03`, 12 paired day observations), as was distance in wrong arms (`p<0.005`; Figure 1D).
- Stimulation did not detectably change wake/non-REM/REM proportions (`p>0.05`, 33 paired sessions) or REM episode count/duration (`p>0.8`/`p>0.3`; Figure 2).
- Stimulation arrived after roughly one third of an ongoing ripple, suppressed ripple power/bursting for about one second, and suppressed multi-unit activity for several hundred milliseconds (Figures 3–4).

## Limitations and challenge pass

- The effective animal sample was five, one animal ended on day four, and repeated days/sessions are not independent animal replications.
- The learning effect was moderate, first detectable on day three, and control/test learning curves converged by the last day. Additional training overcame the impairment.
- Stimulation affected hundreds of milliseconds of broad CA1 activity and later ripples, not a decoded experience-specific replay sequence. It therefore supports a causal role for ripple-associated processing but cannot attribute the effect specifically to replay content.
- The within-animal control did not include matched delayed/random stimulation after the same test memory. Maze assignment/order controls reduce but do not eliminate stimulation-specific alternatives.
- Stimulation occurred mostly in non-REM sleep (84%) but also quiet wake (15%); the design cannot assign the behavioral effect to one state.
- Sleep architecture was preserved, but extra-hippocampal consequences and spindle/up-state coordination were not measured.

## Project relevance and falsifiable hypothesis

Maintenance windows can matter even when raw experience remains stored, but benefit must be attributed to selected memory content rather than extra compute. Add sham-call, random-event, corrupted-event, and content-targeted replay arms under equal compute. Reject content-specific replay if sham or random maintenance matches it, or if missed replay can be fully recovered by ordinary retrieval without delayed-task loss.
