## Method and result

Across three experiments, participants studied category–exemplar pairs, repeatedly retrieved half of the items from half of the categories, and later recalled all items. Retrieval practice improved practiced items while impairing related unpracticed items relative to unpracticed categories. Experiment 2 used 48 participants and controlled final-test output interference; Experiment 3 used 64 participants and manipulated competitor strength. The reported impairment persisted for at least 20 minutes and was stronger for high-frequency competitors.

## Limitations and challenge

- The paradigm measures later accessibility, not direct erasure of a trace.
- Word-category effects do not automatically transfer to long-running agents.
- Later context, blocking, and inhibition accounts disagree on the mechanism.
