## Research question, system, and method

Using contextual fear conditioning in 8–12-week-old male mice, the authors varied placement-to-shock interval (PSI) and measured behavioral context discrimination, total c-fos activity, and reactivation of learning-tagged dentate-gyrus cells using TRAP2:Ai14 mice.

## Extracted results

- In the main behavioral comparison (`n=12/group`), freezing and context discrimination depended on PSI; mice generalized at 0/30 seconds and discriminated at PSI at least 180 seconds (Figure 1, pp. 3–4; PSI × context `F(3,88)=63.20`, `p<0.001`).
- Total c-fos-positive cells rose with the duration of the current session and plateaued at different times by hippocampal subregion (Figure 2, pp. 4–5; `n=6–8/group`).
- Contrary to a prediction, retrieval after 30- versus 720-second learning reached similar maximum total c-fos activity; current reexposure duration, not learning history, governed the total count (Figures 3–4, pp. 5–7; `n=7–8/group` and `n=8/group`).
- In TRAP2 mice (`n=14/group`), 720-second learning produced context-specific behavioral and cellular reactivation, whereas 30-second learning showed low reactivation without context difference (Figure 5, pp. 8–9). The PSI effect on reactivation was `F(1,52)=8.91`, `p<0.01`; the PSI × context interaction was not significant (`F(1,52)=1.76`).

## Limitations and challenge pass

- Male mice, fear conditioning, one hippocampal system, and c-fos/TRAP labeling limit generality.
- The authors distinguish total active cells from the reactivated subset; calling every active cell an engram would reverse the interpretation.
- Larger reactivated ensembles correlated with richer learning/precision in this task; the design does not prove that artificially enlarging any ensemble improves memory.
- The reported nonsignificant interaction and multiple post-hoc comparisons make the exact specificity claim weaker than the headline alone suggests.

## Project relevance and falsifiable hypothesis

Insufficient encoding can preserve a coarse response while losing discriminating context. Benchmark memory not only by whether an item is recalled but whether it is applied specifically rather than generalized to similar safe/irrelevant cases. Reject a "more stored items means precision" analogy unless added evidence fields causally reduce false generalization under a fixed context budget.
