# Berry et al. — Dopamine is required for learning and forgetting in Drosophila

- Status: full HTML read; extracted; primary causal animal study

## Method and result

The authors used temporally controlled GAL4/UAS manipulations, functional imaging, and receptor mutants in olfactory conditioning. Blocking selected dopamine-neuron output after learning enhanced later memory expression; stimulating it accelerated loss. Timing controls and cold-shock experiments separated effects on labile and consolidated components. DAMB receptor disruption increased memory expression and impaired reversal learning, while a different dopamine pathway supported acquisition.

## Limitations

- Fly olfactory conditioning and thermogenetic manipulation do not specify a human forgetting controller.
- A behavioral decrement alone can mix access and trace change; the paper's temporal, sensory, and consolidation controls narrow but do not eliminate every alternative.
- Acquisition and forgetting share dopamine but differ by neuron, receptor, timing, and memory component; `dopamine = importance` is invalid.

## Project consequence

Translate only the control principle: retention and accessibility may have distinct, ongoing regulators. Test factorized and reversible policies; do not copy a neurotransmitter into a scalar score.
