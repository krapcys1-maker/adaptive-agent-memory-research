# Bekinschtein et al. — A retrieval-specific mechanism of adaptive forgetting in the mammalian brain

- Status: full HTML read; extracted; primary causal animal study
- Version read: Nature Communications 9:4660, 2018
- Source: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-018-07128-7
- Full text: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-018-07128-7

## Method and result

Seven rat experiments adapted retrieval-induced forgetting to object-location exploration. Retrieval practice impaired a competing object memory relative to time and interfering-encoding controls. Additional experiments tested competition dependence, alternate cues, 24-hour durability, medial-prefrontal muscimol, and declining control demand. In Experiment 1, the primary comparison used 12 animals and reported large within-animal effects; the authors state the full pattern occurred in 88% of rats studied.

Exact locators: Abstract; Results Figures 1–5; Methods “Experimental design” and “Statistical analysis.”

## Limitations

- Individual experiments used small within-subject animal samples.
- Novel-object preference is an indirect behavioral memory measure.
- Prefrontal dependence supports control involvement but the proposed downstream RAC1 pathway remains speculation in this paper.
- The context account and other retrieval explanations remain relevant even when blocking controls are passed.

## Project consequence

An adaptive-forgetting policy needs a demonstrated competing-task benefit and a neighbor-memory cost curve. Suppression without measured benefit is not adaptive.
