## Method and result

The authors tested more than 900 high-school students across categorized word lists that varied in list length and items per category. Encoding was held constant while the first recall test was either unaided or supplied category names. Cued recall exceeded noncued recall; the advantage grew with list length and decreased with more items per category. Category access and access to items within an accessed category behaved as separable components.

Exact locators: abstract and pp. 381–382 for the storage/access hypothesis and test-time manipulation; pp. 389–390 for the component analysis.

## Limitations and interpretation

- Immediate categorized-word recall does not establish that every failed memory remains permanently stored.
- The manipulation demonstrates recoverability under an added cue, not the physical substrate of the trace.
- Recognition and cued recall change task information and should not be treated as pure measures of storage.

## Project consequence

When a direct-ID or alternate-cue probe recovers an intact record, classify the original miss as an accessibility failure. Do not infer data loss from one unsuccessful query.
