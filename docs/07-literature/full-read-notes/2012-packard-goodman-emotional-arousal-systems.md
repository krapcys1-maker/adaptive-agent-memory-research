## Research question and evidence class

The review asks whether stress/anxiety changes the relative use of hippocampal cognitive/spatial memory and dorsal-striatal stimulus-response/habit memory in animals and humans.

## Extracted claims

- Reviewed animal and human dual-solution studies often show acute/chronic stress or anxiogenic manipulation biasing behavior toward habit strategies, sometimes by impairing competing hippocampal cognition (pp. 2–6).
- Basolateral-amygdala manipulations are presented as a mediator of emotional modulation across systems in animal studies (pp. 3–4).
- Human cortisol findings conflict: some support an inverted-U account, and hydrocortisone alone can differ from combined cortisol/noradrenergic activation. The review says the effect may depend on both cortisol and norepinephrine (pp. 5–6).
- The authors explicitly note that human amygdala activation had not yet been studied in relation to relative strategy use and that acute-stress morphology explanations remained unresolved (p. 6).

## Limitations and challenge pass

- A 2012 selective review is not a current systematic/meta-analytic estimate.
- "Emotion," stress, anxiety, cortisol, norepinephrine, and arousal are not interchangeable.
- Strategy shifts can preserve task success while changing flexibility; simple accuracy can miss this failure.
- Animal BLA/striatal manipulations cannot establish subjective emotional experience in an LLM.
