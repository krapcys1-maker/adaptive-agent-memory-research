## 3. Per-hypothesis novelty verdicts

Verdict labels: **published** (the claim itself is already tested), **partly published** (components or neighbours are tested, the exact claim is not), **untested in what I reached** (not found; may be unmeasured rather than novel in the sense of an unprecedented idea).

### V-H1 compact associative traces preserve low-priority but later-relevant experiences at equal budget
- **Verdict: partly published; the exact test is untested in what I reached.**
- The mechanism (concept graph indexing raw text; PPR or spreading activation to reach it) is not novel: at abstract level HippoRAG / HippoRAG 2 (knowledge graph plus Personalized PageRank) and MRAgent (Cue-Tag-Content graph with active reconstruction) fit it. HeLa-Mem, Auto-Dreamer and MemOS are related (an episodic co-activation graph, a learned offline consolidator that reads linked source trajectories, provenance-linked memory units) but their abstracts do not describe a concept graph over raw text, so their mechanism is not confirmed. Do not present "trace pointing to archive" as a new architecture.
- What I did not find: a benchmark or experiment where items are first labelled or made low-priority at write time and become relevant only after a later goal change, scored at fixed active-context budget against deletion, summary and RAG over the same archive.
- Warning from this repository and from the literature: graph expansion hurt Recall@5 here (ASSOC-E3), second-hop drifted (Entry L), and 2601.00821 found a 1-hop semantic graph did not close the gap to verbatim chunks. A trace used only as a *pointer* with no expansion is the version not yet contradicted.
- Still informative even if not novel: a "pointer-only trace vs. raw-RAG-over-archive" comparison. A key control is a RAG arm that indexes the entire raw archive but only injects 4K tokens, because the relevant question is whether a *trace* beats a plain retrieval index over the same archive at the same budget. If it does not, the repo records a clean negative that the literature (2610.24671, "RAG in a Trenchcoat") predicts.

### V-H2 re-evaluating importance as goals change beats static scores
- **Verdict: partly published.**
- Outcome-driven utility updating exists (MemRL, Oblivion, MemoryBank). Write-time static scoring exists (Generative Agents, A-MAC, Nemori). Goal-conditioned retrieval at query time exists (STITCH, MemGuide, Goal-Mem; snippets only).
- Not found: explicit re-scoring of stored items on goal change, and, crucially, a comparison against goal-conditioned *query-time* retrieval over static scores. That comparison decides whether stored-score re-evaluation is needed at all.
- Informative regardless: three arms (static score; re-evaluated score; static score plus goal-conditioned retrieval). Include the repo's own STALE-E1 caution: supersession is a separate problem from relevance and should not be conflated in the same arm.

### V-H3 cross-domain transfer via associative memory
- **Verdict: partly published (transfer in general), untested for association-mediated transfer.**
- Reported transfer results are mixed: modest average gains (+3.7% in 2604.14004, snippet), dependence on abstract meta-knowledge rather than raw traces, interference needing a gate (Agent KB), and limited content-transfer benefit beyond no-memory (Memory Transplants, snippet).
- Informative regardless: a transfer task with a known shared structure plus a surface-similar distractor; compare associative links, plain RAG and strategy-level memory (ReasoningBank-style). This isolates "genuine task-relevant association vs plausible-sounding but unsupported connection", which the vision itself flags.
- Lowest-confidence row (mostly snippets).

### V-H4 behavioural persistence versus plain retrieval
- **Verdict: partly published; the durable, months-scale version is untested in what I reached.**
- Memory-driven adaptation without weights is well established over task streams (ExpeL, Reflexion, Voyager, ReasoningBank, MemRL, Evo-Memory). The comparison "adaptive memory vs conventional retrieval alone on durable behaviour" is where recent evidence is least favourable to complex memory ("RAG in a Trenchcoat", 2610.24671).
- Informative regardless: interventions the vision already lists (remove one memory, restore an earlier state), reported with seeds. Because durability is slow and expensive, start with a replayed stream where the same agent is evaluated at several memory ages, and keep model and prompt pinned.

### V-H5 efficiency (comparable or better performance with fewer retrieved tokens / lower total cost)
- **Verdict: published as a claim type; not novel.**
- Nearly every system paper above claims large token reductions (Mem0 >90%, LightMem up to 38x, Oblivion 73%, HeLa-Mem "significantly fewer", 2609.04915 83% of full-context quality at 32% of the tokens). The novelty is not the claim but the control: many of these are compared to full context, not to budget-matched raw RAG.
- 2610.24671 finds no consistent advantage over strong raw-history RAG; Retain-or-Consolidate finds retention better under loose budgets and consolidation up to 48% better under tight ones. So the result is budget-conditional and should be reported as a curve over budgets (BudgetBench-style sweep), not at one point.
- Informative regardless: full-lifecycle cost per evaluated task including write-time and re-evaluation calls, which most papers leave out of the headline.

## 4. Contrary or cautionary evidence

1. **Verbatim beats extraction.** An (2601.00821): chunks beat extracted artifacts by 15.9 points (LoCoMo) and 22.0 points (LongMemEval-S); a 1-hop graph did not close the gap; recommendation is to supplement verbatim text, not replace it (abs). Already in the repo; consistent with ledger Entry H.
2. **Explicit memory shows no consistent edge over raw-history RAG.** Zhou et al., 2610.24671 (abs): six systems, LongMemEval-S and BEAM-100K, only localised gains for certain question types and tight budgets; some systems lose evidence at construction, others keep it but fail to retrieve it; construction can take hours per conversation.
3. **Extra machinery does not earn its cost on standard benchmarks.** "RAG in a Trenchcoat: When Minimal Memory Is Enough for Agentic Systems, and When It Isn't" (Liu, Li, Xu, Zhou, Rabbani, Song, Zhang; NeurIPS 2026 poster 151457, https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/poster/151457, OpenReview forum id aUiKcKSG3t; no arXiv id found): a minimal chained-notes plus hybrid BM25/cosine design uses about 5-7K prompt tokens and reaches parity with prior SOTA on LongMemEval-S and BEAM; extraction, supersession, reflection and graph synthesis add little there. Adversarial probes show the exceptions: atom-fact extractors win rule application (+45pp), block summaries win pattern induction (+33pp). Lesson for this project: the winning design depends on the mechanism required, so the experiment must name the mechanism (delayed relevance) and not assume a general benefit (abs of poster page).
4. **Retrieval, not storage, is the dominant failure.** EvalMem (2609.22231): retrieval is the most frequently blamed layer (per-layer defect rates 22.1% for retrieval, 7.7% for encoding, 6.5% for generation, as reported in the abstract); a recall-first agentic RAG raised evidence recall on LoCoMo from 70.2% to 95.6% (abs). This supports the vision's premise that the item is present but not surfaced, and also says a better retriever may be the cheaper fix than a new representation.
5. **Consolidation is budget-conditional.** Retain-or-Consolidate (2607.17545): retention is preferable under loose budgets; consolidation helps under tight ones, and local rewriting is worse than merge or cross-note abstraction (abs).
6. **Budget sweeps change rankings and tokenizer error can distort them.** BudgetBench (2609.13149): non-monotonic quality curves, single-budget evaluation hides operating points, the local slice was near-null, and an early tokenizer approximation undercounted prompts (abs). MemoryAgentBench compute-matched snippet: RAG beats long context at 4K but saturates and degrades at high budget. Relevant to the vision's "equal or explicitly measured budgets" rule: use exact tokenization.
7. **Transfer is not guaranteed.** Memory Transplants and Agent KB snippets: limited content-transfer benefit beyond no-memory and need for an interference gate (snip, low confidence).
8. **Deferred relevance is hard even for frontier models.** PM-Bench: best model 65.1% F1, no single strategy dominates (abs). Different construct (intentions), but a caution for any "later it becomes relevant" task.
9. **Graph retrieval can underperform.** A GraphRAG survey summarised in a search result reports it can underperform vanilla RAG in realistic settings (snip, source not identified). Together with the repo's ASSOC-E3 and Entry L, this is the mildest-supported but consistent signal. One more abstract (2608.28978, "Selective Forgetting: A Graph-Based Memory Framework for Long-Term LLM Agents", Rusu, Khanzadeh, Alalfi, Aug 2026) reports that its graph pruned 9.8% of nodes and did not beat a flat vector baseline (token F1 0.417 versus 0.468) (abs, from the independent check).
10. **Importance scoring reliability: not found.** I looked and did not find a paper that evaluates how well write-time LLM importance scores predict later utility. A-MAC reports content-type prior as the strongest factor, which hints that simple priors dominate learned or LLM-judged utility, but the abstract does not give the ablation (abs). Treat this as an open gap, not as evidence that scoring works.
11. **Durable adaptation / drift: not found.** I found no multi-month study of memory-augmented agents. Self-evolving memory risks are discussed in governance work (SSGM, snip) without results I could verify.

## 6. What I could not verify

- No paper body, appendix or repository was read. All claims are from abstract pages (through a summarising fetch tool) or search snippets. Numbers quoted (percentages, token reductions) are the authors' reported figures as relayed by those pages.
- Whether Oblivion, HeLa-Mem, MRAgent, FadeMem or Auto-Dreamer keep raw records after forgetting or consolidation: stated nowhere in the abstracts I saw. This is the crux for "operational forgetting vs deletion" and for V-H1, and needs a body or code read.
- Whether LoCoMo, BEAM, EvalMem's DynaMem-Bench, Evo-Memory or AMA-Bench contain any item whose relevance arises only after a goal change. The abstracts are silent; silence is not absence.
- Whether any goal-conditioned retrieval paper (STITCH, MemGuide, Goal-Mem) already recovers dissimilar-but-needed memories; I saw only snippets that frame the problem the other way round.
- The ACL 2026 status of Nemori, and whether an arXiv version of "RAG in a Trenchcoat" exists (none found).
- The Google "sleep framework" blog dated 2026-10-27 and the two 2026 "language models need sleep" papers (2605.26099, 2606.03979): seen in snippets only, and they concern weight-level consolidation, which is outside the "no weight changes" scope.
- Hindsight (named in the vision), Memory-R1-style RL memory managers, and any work on wrongly reinforced frequently-accessed memories: not searched or not found.
- The statements about Generative Agents' scoring, MemGPT's recall/archival tiers and Graphiti validity windows are from background knowledge or the repo notes, not from pages fetched here.
- The repository's own findings (ASSOC-E3, Entry L, Entry H, STALE-E1) were taken from the review brief; I did not open the ledger or experiment records.
- Tool-call budget was exceeded modestly (about 53 calls against a target of about 45) to close the required-system list; no marginal findings were left obviously on the table, but the Section 1 "not reached" list is where a second pass should start.
