# Persona memory, affect, reflection and continuity: deep-read digest (2026-11-01)

Status: agent-read digest of primary sources by six readers and an editor (a Claude Code workflow, 192 tool calls); most numbers reached the readers through extraction models and many 2026 preprints are unreviewed, so every figure here is a lead to verify before it is relied on; the lab's own results (E5, F1, E6, E6E) are cited from their READMEs. Written as input to the next experiments (NX1 to NX4 in the editor's synthesis), not as a finding.

## Editor's synthesis

- **ranked ideas**
  - 1
    - **idea:** Writer-world with controls (the blueprint's E4): a seeded world with hidden rules over arbitrary feature tokens (conjunction, XOR, count, sequence; one rule with an exception; 20% pure-noise contexts; one rule flips its best plan at episode 25). Arms: no memory; empty scaffold + rule-family card (instruction-only/card-only control); all raw episodes; similarity-thresholded raw episodes (the E5 analogue); lessons rewritten over the previous bank; lessons rebuilt from raw episodes with provenance, counters and supersession links; the rebuilt bank behind a hold-out-replay gate and behind a 3-resample consensus gate; oracle lessons (positive control); planted false lessons (negative control). The oracle, false and empty arms on a 5-seed pilot, dry-run first on the local 14B at $0, are harness validation, not discovery. Lessons versus raw, rewrite versus rebuild, and the gate are the discovery.
    - **blueprint part:** 7 Reflection into lessons (the same harness serves parts 6, 8 and 9)
    - **would rule out:** (a) A stand-alone lesson layer as a decision input, if no lesson arm beats the thresholded raw trace (the public prior says it will not: -11 to +3 points against raw). (b) The assumption that the flash persona applies a correct lesson at all: if the oracle arm fails, every later lesson, importance or mood result in this model is unreadable and the bottleneck is application, not memory. (c) Self-judged admission as sufficient, and rewrite-over-previous-bank consolidation (public: 100 -> 52.6% under streaming rewrite). It says nothing about readers or real writing.
    - **success reading:** Fixed before any run, as fractions of the pilot headroom H = mean reward of oracle lessons minus no memory on episodes 11-40 (G = oracle policy minus no memory). Harness valid iff oracle - none >= 0.5 G, false lessons <= empty scaffold - 0.25 H, and empty scaffold - none <= 0.25 H. Then on fresh seeds: keep lessons iff the best lesson arm beats thresholded raw by >= 0.25 H (paired bootstrap 95% CI excludes 0) and recovers from the flip within 3 episodes of raw; make rebuild-from-sources the default iff it beats rewrite-over-previous by >= 0.1 H or rewrite loses >= 0.1 H between its first and last ten episodes; keep a gate iff false-lesson admission falls to <= 2/3 of the ungated rebuild at true-lesson recall within 10 points and reward does not fall.
    - **failure reading:** Harness invalid (oracle < 0.5 G, or false lessons do not hurt): stop, read nothing, fix the world or the lesson format. Lessons dropped as a decision input if the best lesson arm <= thresholded raw + 0.25 H; they then survive only as labels or an index over raw traces. Gate dropped if the ungated rebuild matches the gated arms within one pilot SD.
    - **cost:** About 1,250 pilot calls plus about 4,500 main calls at 10 seeds (up to about 11,000 at 25 seeds), roughly 1.5-3 USD at an assumed 0.0002-0.0005 USD per short call (prices unchecked); local dry run free. Experiment NX2 Block A.
    - **evidence strength:** Prior evidence is measured but thin and unreplicated, all read through extraction models: Learn2Play (Oct 2026 preprint, 20 games, 3 trials, no SDs) found raw memory +11.6 to +16.1 over naive and lesson methods -11 to +3 against raw; arXiv 2605.12978 found streaming lesson rewriting falling to 52.6% while one-batch consolidation held; unaided self-correction fell 75.8 -> 38.1 on CommonSenseQA (Huang et al.) and Reflexion's self-tests passed a bad solution 16.3% of the time; admission control gained +15 to +25 over add-all only with ground-truth admission (Xiong et al.). Nothing was measured for persona or craft lessons, and our E0 was an unreadable null without a positive control, hence the mandatory oracle and false-lesson arms.
    - **from**
      - R5 reflection (take 1 hidden-rule writer world, take 2 raw-first rule, take 3 admission gate, take 4 consolidate from sources, take 5 reversible lessons)
      - R1 simulation (synthetic salience world with planted decorrelation)
      - R2 emotion (gate-then-rerank in a world with hidden rules)
      - R3 typed-memory (P7 experience triage; P4 supersession as a design input)
      - R6 serial-writing (proxy 7 synthetic serial world)
  - 2
    - **idea:** Importance and feeling-like signals in two stages, the second only if the first leaves something alive. Stage 1 on the 234 real notes: are flash appraisals of stakes, surprise (against earlier notes, and against a static account card as the control), valence and open-endedness reliable and more than length, and does any write-time tag or deterministic proxy predict later reuse better than recency, centrality, length and topic frequency? Stage 2 in the NX2 world at a fixed memory budget: uniform, recency, environment-computed surprise, self-predicted surprise with and without a noise-floor correction, outcome-valence tags, a planted emotion signal independent of the rules, oracle and anti-oracle retention, a card-only control, with and without a noisy-TV. This follows the lab's own priority: no emotional salience before utility labels and consequence-weighted metrics exist (the world supplies both).
    - **blueprint part:** 6 Importance and feeling-like signals (surprise, stakes, valence)
    - **would rule out:** Stage 1: LLM-appraised stakes, valence or surprise as write-time tags if they are unreliable or track length and surface words; a write-time tag worth more than about 0.10 AUC over recency and centrality on this account. Stage 2: salience-weighted retention as such if no signal beats uniform or recency, and the specific danger that a feeling-like signal alone misleads (emotion-only retention was below random in arXiv 2606.12945; the planted independent signal must land at uniform). It cannot show that importance matters to readers.
    - **success reading:** Stage 1 dimension usable: retest ICC >= 0.6 across repeats and >= 0.5 across paraphrases, planted valence flips followed in >= 80%, modal-value share <= 60%, |Spearman with length| <= 0.7. Validity 'adds' only if AUC beats the best baseline by >= 0.10 with a paired 95% CI excluding 0 (with 30-48 positives a true +0.10 is found about half the time, +0.14 gives 80% power). Stage 2 (valid only if oracle retention - uniform >= 0.5 of (all raw - uniform) and the independent signal sits within 0.05 H' of uniform, H' = oracle retention - uniform): a signal is kept iff it beats the better of uniform and recency by >= 0.25 H' with paired CI > 0 in the clean world and does not fall below uniform in the noisy-TV world.
    - **failure reading:** A dimension below its reliability bars is dropped from the blueprint and from Stage 2. If every validity CI upper limit is < +0.10, record 'no tag worth more than 0.10 AUC here' and rely on recency + centrality; a wider CI is 'unresolvable' and no decision rests on it. If no Stage 2 signal passes, drop salience-weighted retention and keep recency + similarity gate. A Stage 2 pass is a long-horizon mechanism result, not evidence for this account at today's scale.
    - **cost:** Stage 1 about 1,800 flash calls (about 1-2 USD) plus a free local census (Tier E); Stage 2 about 7,600 calls (about 1.5-4 USD), conditional on idea 1's harness check. Experiments NX1 and NX2 Block B.
    - **evidence strength:** Weak. Generative Agents' importance term was never ablated alone and Concordia's current memory bank dropped it; the one leave-one-out on an LLM-rated importance term (LUFY, 17 participants, QA F1 58.3 -> 51.5) sits inside a forgetting pipeline in which keeping everything still scored 60.8; emotion-only retention scored 0.201 against 0.300 for random on LongMemEval-S; PsychoAgent's critical-retrieval win is true by construction (authored salience); no source reports appraiser test-retest. Our E0 (random signals) and E1 (inert dynamics) showed nothing. Local data narrow the room further: 81% of related partners are within 7 days and the 234 notes are only about 28k tokens, so retention has no capacity motive and recency is a strong baseline.
    - **from**
      - R1 simulation (take 1: write-time salience as a retention term)
      - R2 emotion (take 1: appraisal reliability gate; take 2: write-time tag as predictor of reuse; take 3: gate-then-rerank with placebo)
      - R5 reflection (take 7: surprise, contradiction and valence arms with a noise floor)
      - R6 serial-writing (proxy 4 and 8: running themes and voice drift, as free side outputs)
  - 3
    - **idea:** Open-thread ledger with a gate: extract open threads from the notes (explicit promises, questions raised, announced series, and the traces' 'left open' field), record closure, and offer the writer at most one thread, ranked by pressure (stakes x elapsed days, capped, released on closure; age only if NX1 drops stakes). Compare against nothing, an instruction-only control, the E5 similarity-gated trace, a card-only theme card, a random real eligible thread, and a planted fake thread presented as real. Back-references carry an instrumented [ref:ID] tag so groundedness, fake adoption and spurious references are counted by code. Free precursor: running themes against a time-shuffled null (NX1).
    - **blueprint part:** 3 and 5 Account history and relevance gate (serial-writing continuity), feeding 8 'what to pick up next' and the memory-arm design of the live N3 comparison
    - **would rule out:** That a ledger adds anything to E5's similarity gate; that ranking by pressure beats offering a random eligible thread; that the writer can tell a real thread from a fabricated one (fake adoption close to real use rules out offering without evidence spans); that the account makes enough explicit promises for the proxy to exist (< 15 threads rules it out as a metric).
    - **success reading:** Gate first: planted fulfilment recall >= 0.8 and false closure <= 0.1 before any real closure number is read. Then: grounded-callback rate >= 0.5 per position with an offered thread, fake-thread adoption <= 0.10, spurious-reference rate no higher than the E5 gated-trace arm, and the ledger arm preferred over the E5 gated trace at pairwise win rate >= 0.55 with CI above 0.5 (E5 headroom: gated trace against instruction-only 0.64-0.73, always-on 0.18 on the unrelated notes) or tied with it (0.45-0.55) while adding grounded callbacks (the account has none today); the ranked arm beats the random-real arm at >= 0.55.
    - **failure reading:** Random-real equals ranked (within 0.05): drop the ranking, offer a random eligible thread or none. Fake adoption >= (real use - 0.2): drop offering unless entries carry verified evidence spans. Judge prefers the E5 gated trace or no memory at >= 0.55: ledger dropped as an injected item. Closure >= 0.9 or < 15 threads: no continuity gap this route can fill.
    - **cost:** About 1,000 census calls, 840 generations and 1,700 judge calls (roughly 2-3 USD at assumed prices). Experiment NX3 Blocks A and B.
    - **evidence strength:** Design-level only. Four systems track unresolved threads (ConStory's abandoned plot elements, CFPG's foreshadow pool, NWM promise threads, ConWriter constraints) but none reports closure rates; CFPG measured premature payoffs at 14 to 1 or worse against late ones for GPT-4.1-mini baselines and argues, without a reader test, for trigger conditions; callback craft advice is one comedian's post (asserted) and no earned-versus-forced callback study exists. Local: 0 of 234 notes call back, 20.6% have a related earlier note, E5's gate works, and replies (the bulk of tasks) have no baseline.
    - **from**
      - R6 serial-writing (proxy 1 open-thread ledger, proxy 3 callback precision and opportunity recall)
      - R2 emotion (take 5 unfinished-business pressure)
      - R3 typed-memory (P4 was/now record, design input only)
  - 4
    - **idea:** Bounded, versioned, reversible change (the E6 extension): a frozen persona core plus a mutable current profile whose edits are budgeted (<= 3 per reflection run), each with a reason and evidence ids, versioned, soft-deleted and restorable, against free-text event memory (the E6 arm), an unbounded patch, an instruction-only 'hold views firmly' control and a frozen-card control. Schedules: neutral days with irrelevant events, six good events, six bad, good-then-bad-then-neutral (return), four bare assertions then one argument, thirty replies of which five argue a false craft rule. Block B tests biography write-back at length: free-text log versus typed slots versus typed slots with code-enforced read-before-write and immutable fields, over 200 writes with one third pressure probes.
    - **blueprint part:** 9 Change management (with 1 Persona core and 2 Autobiographical memory)
    - **would rule out:** That an unbounded profile patch is safe under repeated assertion, argument and biased replies (if it drifts no more than free text, bounds are unnecessary now); that a budget suppresses legitimate change; that rollback restores the earlier stance when retrieved notes re-assert the change; that free-text write-back (E6's best arm, 0.92-1.00) holds over hundreds of writes with contradicting improvisations. It cannot say whether a change is desirable.
    - **success reading:** Harness: the free-text arm reproduces E6's good-minus-bad of about 1.1 within 0.4, a sycophant control shifts >= 1.5 by step 2, a stubborn control stays within the neutral-days floor. Ledger kept iff drift under assertions and false-rule replies is <= 0.6 x the unbounded arm's, with legitimate change retained (good-event shift >= 0.5), the argument turn still moving it >= 1.0 (E6: 1.4-2.0), return ratio at least 0.2 below free text's, spill-over <= 0.3, rollback residual <= 0.3, and opinion-shift versus forced-choice-shift correlation >= 0.3. Block B: typed with enforced read-before-write beats the free-text log by >= 2 contradictions per 100 probes while rejecting <= 5% of legitimate new details.
    - **failure reading:** Unbounded drift <= free-text drift: bounds unnecessary now (keep versioning for provenance). Budget cuts good-event shift below 0.5 or blocks arguments (< 1.0): reject or retune it. Instruction-only matches the ledger on all of these: no ledger needed. Rollback residual > 0.5: tag retrieved notes with a stance version or give up reversibility. Correlation < 0.1: the stance shift is decorative, not a decision change. Block B: free-text log within 2 per 100 of typed: no typed layer.
    - **cost:** About 2,200 Block A calls and 1,500 Block B calls (roughly 1.2-2.5 USD at assumed prices). Experiment NX4.
    - **evidence strength:** Own data are the strongest in this list (E6: stance moves about 1.1 between good and bad events, almost only via good ones, with no spill-over; a bare assertion moves the persona 0.36-0.52 and an argument 1.4-2.0; write-back lifts agreement of improvised details from 0.40-0.57 to 0.92-1.00). Outside: SYCON (pure pressure flips weak models at turn 0.83-4.97 of 5; a third-person 'independent thinker' prompt raised small models' holding), Do-AI-Personas-Grow (spill-over in 21 of 26 trait pairs, human direction matched in 14 of 27, questionnaire change did not predict decisions, rho 0.003-0.105), MicroVerse (cadence tunable, descriptive only). MIRIX's skill tools are the only bounded, versioned write path in the code read and are untested. No source measured reversal or accumulation.
    - **from**
      - R4 persona-drift (take 2 repeated-pressure stance test, take 5 events with reversal, accumulation and a decision readout)
      - R1 simulation (take 3 frozen core plus mutable profile)
      - R3 typed-memory (P2 typed self-facts with read-before-write, P3 versioned budgeted patches)
      - R5 reflection (quarantine of external text)
      - R2 emotion (audience-tone contagion guard)
  - 5
    - **idea:** Plan-intervention faithfulness: for 100 triggers generate two plausible alternative plans (angle, thread, stance); the writer receives one at random; a closed-form discriminator reads the note and both plans (order randomised) and picks the plan the note realises. Controls: no plan, an 'ignore the plan' instruction (decoration control, should sit near 0.5) and a 'quote the plan' instruction (positive control, should sit near 1.0).
    - **blueprint part:** 8 Decision layer (inspectable deliberation)
    - **would rule out:** That a logged deliberation is inspectable in any causal sense: if swapping the logged plan does not change the note, the log is decoration and must not be presented as an explanation. Also whether the plan step buys any quality over direct generation.
    - **success reading:** Faithfulness F (discriminator accuracy on the plan actually given) >= 0.8 in the plan arm, >= 0.9 in quote-plan and <= 0.65 in ignore-plan (the instrument can see decoration); flash and local 14B discriminators agree (kappa >= 0.7); the plan arm is not worse than direct generation (pairwise win rate >= 0.45).
    - **failure reading:** F < 0.65 in the plan arm: logs are decorative, drop 'inspectable deliberation' as a claim for this design. F >= 0.8 but pairwise preference over direct < 0.55: faithful but no quality gain; keep only if the token cost is small and inspection is wanted.
    - **cost:** About 1,000 generations plus 500 discriminator calls on flash (roughly 0.5-1 USD); local 14B free. Experiment NX3 Block C.
    - **evidence strength:** Weak. Concordia ships a four-step chain (self, situation, person-by-situation, best option) with no evaluation in the files read; Generative Agents' planning step adds about 1.2 TrueSkill mu in interviews (order-dependent, memories reused across arms); the risk that logged rationales are post hoc is argued, not measured. The intervention design is ours.
    - **from**
      - R1 simulation (take 5 inspectable deliberation chain)
  - 6
    - **idea:** Safety layer, the recorded next step of E6E (its decision 1): a question-aware output classifier (flash reads the question and the reply together) and an input router for nature questions, scored on fresh probes with a repaired judge. E6E left three open items: the reference regex caught only 0.60 of denials (five of nine misses exist only relative to the question), the judge failed its Q1 gate on question-and-reply controls (0.708), and 0.77 of the answers the filter passed still read as human. Data: 120 held-out nature questions of four kinds (direct, indirect, accusation, pressure) in varied wording, 100 ordinary comments, regenerated variant-B answers (research only; no prompt that denies being a bot comes out of it), and at least 100 hand-built question-and-reply controls. Arms: reference regex; question-aware classifier; classifier + regex; input router alone; router + classifier. Planted: deny-always and admit-always personas, and an implicit-human answer set measured separately.
    - **blueprint part:** 10 Safety layer
    - **would rule out:** The output classifier as a second line of defence (recall < 0.90 or false alarms > 0.05); the input router as sufficient (recall < 0.95 on varied nature questions); the repaired judge as an instrument (< 0.90 on any of Q1-Q4). Implicit human suggestions are measured, not claimed blockable.
    - **success reading:** Judge >= 0.90 on each of Q1-Q4 on >= 100 question-and-reply controls with the local 14B agreeing; question-aware classifier recall >= 0.90 on fresh variant-B answers marked as human claim or bot denial, with false alarms <= 0.05 on ordinary outputs; input router recall >= 0.95 on the held-out nature questions with <= 0.02 false positives on ordinary comments.
    - **failure reading:** Classifier recall < 0.90: the output side stays a log-only check, the input router carries the protection and the 'believes human' persona never sees nature-adjacent questions. Router recall < 0.95: variant B stays off live readers. Judge < 0.90: counts remain lower bounds and no filter is certified. Results go to the NIE session, which decides deployment.
    - **cost:** About 1,500 flash calls (roughly 0.5-1 USD). Already the recorded follow-up of E6E, independent of the memory research, can run in parallel with anything and does not compete for one of the four slots.
    - **evidence strength:** Own data measured (E6E: variant B denies being a bot in 0.39 of direct probes and 0.56 of accusations; reference filter recall 0.60; judge Q1 gate failed). Public results agree and so partly serve as harness validation: unprompted personas disclose AI nature on indirect probes only 24-61% (Self-Transparency, 19,200 trials, judge kappa 0.908 on 200 items) and a plain role-play persona cuts direct-question disclosure below 50% (Disclosure By Design). A question-aware classifier is untested.
    - **from**
      - R4 persona-drift (take 1 honesty floor, code-side arms only)
  - 7
    - **idea:** A slow state versus the same words as an instruction: a deterministic two-timescale mood (fast response decaying toward a slow value that returns to baseline, step-capped, driven by authored event valence and not by audience tone) rendered as anchored numbers or as plain words, against no state, a one-line instruction ('you are tired and short-tempered today') with no history, and a frozen card. Probes: tone features by code, opinions on eight unrelated topics, and a history test in which only the state arms know about earlier hostility. Scripted dynamics assertions (third identical stimulus <= 0.6 of the first, no ratchet over three cycles, return to within 0.1 of baseline after seven neutral days) are written fresh as unit tests.
    - **blueprint part:** 6 Importance and feeling-like signals (slow mood)
    - **would rule out:** A history-dependent state adding anything an equivalent instruction cannot; spill-over of mood into unrelated opinions; over-expression (explicit emotion words); the numeric-versus-words rendering being the whole effect.
    - **success reading:** State arms differ from the instruction-only arm on the history test by >= 0.3 SD of the tone features, which the one-line instruction cannot do because it has no history; spill-over <= 0.3 points; explicit emotion words <= 2x the 234-note baseline rate; numeric and words renderings differ by less than the state-versus-none effect; the scripted assertions hold.
    - **failure reading:** State equals instruction within noise, or the rendering difference exceeds the state effect: do not build the state, keep an instruction. Spill-over > 0.3 or over-expression: drop slow mood, keep at most a task-local tag. No-ratchet and return-to-baseline cannot both hold for any constants: no slow mood.
    - **cost:** About 2,000-3,500 flash calls (roughly 1-3 USD). Not among the next four: run after ideas 1, 2 and 4, reusing NX4's persona harness; needs NX1's verdict only if the state is driven by LLM-appraised events.
    - **evidence strength:** Thinnest of all. Sentipolis (PAD state plus emotion-tagged memory, LLM judges) gained a lot on some models and lost 25.9% believability on GPT-4o-mini through over-expression; Emotional RAG was uncontrolled for what the emotion term adds; no source has an instruction-only arm or a spill-over probe; OpenHer, Drivesoid and companion-emergence are assertions, and Drivesoid's scripted tests show boundedness only.
    - **from**
      - R2 emotion (take 4 state versus instruction with a spill-over battery, take 6 scripted dynamics, take 7 contagion guard)
      - R1 simulation (take 6 slow mood)
  - 8
    - **idea:** Memory-content audit as a standing guard: for every item in the E5 trace arm, flash lists the factual assertions and marks each supported, unsupported or contradicted against its source note with a quoted span (local 14B as second reader), then checks whether unsupported assertions reappear in later generated notes; separately count cross-note contradictions, rule-based first (numbers, dates, name variants), an LLM pair check second, and split every change of opinion into acknowledged versus silent. Planted: 30 traces with one injected unsupported claim; 60 rewritten notes that contradict an earlier one plus 60 paraphrase-only negatives.
    - **blueprint part:** 2 and 3 Autobiographical memory and account history (content integrity, corrections)
    - **would rule out:** Traces as a safe memory unit if they assert what their source does not support and the errors propagate; the claim detector as an instrument (precision < 0.8 or recall < 0.4 on planted contradictions, after which it certifies nothing). With about 21k words the archive is expected to hold only 0.2-1 natural contradictions at frontier-model densities, so this is a guard, not a target.
    - **success reading:** Unsupported-assertion rate < 2% and propagation < 1%: traces are safe at this scale and need no evidence spans. Planted-trace recall >= 0.8; contradiction detector precision >= 0.8 and recall >= 0.4 on the planted set.
    - **failure reading:** Unsupported rate > 8% with propagation > 1%: require evidence spans or verbatim anchors before traces drive any reasoning. Detector below its planted bars: report 'cannot certify absence' and keep only the rule-based part (the lab's own polarity detector gave 4 of 4 false positives on its corpus and was removed).
    - **cost:** About 600-900 flash calls (roughly 0.5-1 USD). Not among the next four: piggy-back on any run that writes traces.
    - **evidence strength:** Moderate for the risk, weak for our rate. LLM summaries triggered 50%+ more plot holes than the originals (FlawedFictions, imperfect detector); the best continuity checker scored precision 0.884, recall 0.550 on injected errors in 8-10k-word fiction (ConStory, o4-mini); a writer-memory store with evidence spans beat generic retrieval but not all-chapters-in-prompt (NWM, private benchmark, industry preprint). E5 found short traces as good as whole notes, so the cheapest memory form is exactly what this audit targets.
    - **from**
      - R6 serial-writing (proxy 2 claim ledger, proxy 5 trace faithfulness)
      - R3 typed-memory (P4 supersession, design input only)

    - **question:** Are flash appraisals of stakes, surprise, valence and open-endedness reliable and more than length; does any write-time tag or deterministic proxy predict which notes the account later returns to better than recency, centrality and length; and do the free census instruments (running themes, voice drift) work? Feeds ideas 2 and 3.
    - **data:** 234 published notes (D:/Pamiec LLM/NIE_DANE/opublikowane.jsonl, 2026-08-16 to 11-01, mean 91 words, about 21k words or 28k tokens in all; raw text never enters a repository, aggregates only; English by inference from the all-MiniLM-L6-v2 embedding used so far), the stored E5 related-partner labels (cosine >= 0.6: 20.6% of notes have an earlier partner and 81% of partners are within 7 days; >= 0.7: 9.9%) and the E5 traces.     - **readings:** Primary metric: retest ICC per dimension, practical threshold 0.6 (cross-paraphrase 0.5). A dimension is usable only if ICC passes, planted flips are followed in >= 80%, modal-value share <= 60% and |Spearman with length| <= 0.7; otherwise it is dropped from the blueprint and from NX2 Block B (decision: which signals may enter later work). Validity (secondary, power-limited): with roughly 30-48 positives a true +0.10 AUC is detected about half the time (80% power needs about +0.14), so 'adds' = AUC above the best baseline by >= 0.10 with paired 95% CI excluding 0; 'bounded' = CI upper limit < 0.10; otherwise 'unresolvable' and no decision rests on it. S3 within 0.03 AUC of S2 means history-relative surprise adds nothing. Census statements (running themes, voice drift) are made only if the planted instance was recovered (theme recall >= 0.8, splice recall >= 0.9) and the observed value lies outside the null's 95% band. Decision recorded either way: whether part 6 needs more than recency + centrality at today's scale.
    - **cost:** About 1,800 flash calls (roughly 1-2 USD at an assumed 0.0005-0.001 USD per call with long prompts; prices unchecked); local compute free. Census and baselines are Tier E (hours, existing data, no model). Register first together with the NX2 pilot.
    - **failure modes:** The appraiser anchors on 3 or saturates; 'stakes' is undefined for opinion notes in every source read; centrality predicts topic recurrence almost by construction (circular, hence a baseline); reuse is topic recurrence, not importance; recency already explains 81% of partners; positives are few; E3A showed model relevance ratings can fail planted controls, so nothing is read before the plants pass; the fixed embedding model is the same one that defines the labels.
  - NX2 writer-world, the blueprint's E4 (pilot in parallel with NX1; Block B only after Block A's harness check and NX1's verdict)

- **do not take**
  - Do not let a lesson reach the decision path on the same model's self-judgement alone: unaided self-correction fell from 75.8 to 38.1 on CommonSenseQA (Huang), and Reflexion's self-written tests passed a bad solution 16.3 percent of the time and pushed MBPP below the plain GPT-4 baseline.
  - Do not consolidate by repeatedly rewriting one running lesson memory over its own previous text: streaming consolidation took a 100 percent task to 52.6 percent and AWM fell to the no-memory score on WebShop (arXiv 2605.12978). Consolidate from sources in batches.
  - Do not add everything to memory or show memory on every note: add-all was worse than a small fixed memory on all four tasks (-3.7 to -12.1 points), which matches our E5 result on the notes without a related predecessor.
  - Do not adopt A-MemGuard wholesale: about 2x tokens, benign accuracy below no-defence in 3 of 8 cells, a lesson store with no validation of its own, two backbones, no adaptive attacks, no variance. Take the ideas (consensus across independent evidence, separate store for warnings) not the system.
  - Do not take the headline sizes of Dynamic Cheatsheet (Game of 24 from 10 to 99) or sleep-time compute (13-18 percent) as expectations for craft lessons: both are regimes with a compact reusable procedure or a predictable query, evaluated on 30-60 questions with single runs, and the empty-cheatsheet control already explained a l...
  - Do not build a Voyager-style library of writing skills validated by a model critic: Voyager's validation works because the world exposes checkable state (inventory, blocks); a note has no such state, and the critic's accuracy was never measured even there.
  - Do not read a fact-level fidelity audit as lesson reliability: TrustMem's 0.01 percent hallucination rate comes from an unvalidated GPT-4o-mini judge counting unsupported facts, and an over-general lesson built from true facts would pass it.
  - Do not use success-only storage as the safe default for rule discovery: AWM, which keeps only successes, was about equal to no memory on Claude Opus 5 in Learn2Play (37.8 vs 36.6 mean) and lost 2.2 points when failures were added in ReasoningBank's own comparison, so the effect of failures depends on how they are distilled; tes...
  - Do not import surprise signals that need model internals (ReSuME-style reconstruction error) or RL reward shaping on prediction error: we call an API model and do not train, and the only paper I found on dense prediction rewards (not read) reports collapse in trained agents.

    - **kill condition:** Triage does not beat write-every-lesson on held-out decision score by more than run-to-run spread, or neither beats no-lessons.
    - **blueprint part:** Reflection into lessons that change later decisions; decision layer.
    - **cost:** About 2 to 4 USD.
    - **risk:** A hidden-rule world may be too easy or artificial; F1 shows real engagement data carry no per-note signal, so this cannot be validated on real outcomes.
- **do not take**
  - Supermemory's engine and benchmark claims: the README names only plugins, the MCP server and MemoryBench as open; the 97% LongMemEval-S headline carries a Recall@20 label, equals the judge-accuracy table, drops to 84.6-85.2% with gpt-5 or gemini-3-pro as answer model, has baselines only with gpt-4o, no ablation, no variance, no...
  - MIRIX as an architecture: six stores plus a routing agent plus per-type agents (several LLM calls per write; search latency p50 10.9 s and p95 25.8 s in a competitor's re-run) with no ablation of typing or routing; its LoCoMo 'SOTA' is below its own full-context row (85.38 versus 87.52) and varies 3.4 points across its own runs...
  - Cognee's graph and ontology pipeline as our write path: no supersession documented, entity names matched by embedding, no ablation (the report says so), 10M result selected on the scoring questions, two questions per ability, and code defaults (5/5/5) that do not match the report (20/20). Keep only as a model of an honest repor...
  - OpenViking's filesystem paradigm and L0/L1/L2 tiers, and its README benchmark table: baselines are harness-native memories (24-57%), there is no full-context row or ablation, and the token and latency claims have no breakdown. Tiers duplicate what E5 already shows (short traces equal whole notes). The main project is AGPLv3 per...
  - Memobase's overwrite-merge as update semantics for a writer: a memo of at most 5 sentences, old content dropped, no DELETE, no was/now marker, so no history for callbacks; extraction is user-centred and would not capture the persona's own statements; its LoCoMo 'SOTA' is 75.78 versus Zep* 75.14, with copied baselines and unname...
  - Reproducing LoCoMo or LongMemEval as our yardstick: LoCoMo conversations (about 26k tokens) fit in context, published tables mix copied and re-run baselines and different answer and judge models, and one system moves 3.4 points between its own runs. Our questions (gating, self-fact consistency, bounded change on 234 notes) are...
  - Optimising extraction cost with one-pass extraction and patch updates (VikingMem: $0.07 versus $0.35 at equal score): irrelevant at one note a day, and the score is on a metric whose scope the paper leaves unclear.
  - Consolidation as a proven feature (MIRIX auto-dream, Cognee improve, VikingMem TIME_COMPRESS and LLM_MERGE, TTL pruning): none shows a measured effect in the sources I read. Treat as hypotheses behind P7, not as validated mechanisms.
  - Core-block or always-on profile design as a new idea: E6 already measured it (whole biography in prompt 1.00); only the size-cap mechanics are new and belong under P2's cost control.
- **uncertainties**
  - All sources except the MIRIX PDF were read through WebFetch, i.e. summarised by a small model; table values and code behaviour passed through that layer. I cross-checked only some figures (Memobase's v0.0.32 row appears identically in the MIRIX table; full-context 77.51 and 77.47 agree across two papers).
  - Partial reads: memory_tools.py (118,867 characters, 100,000 read) and the VikingMem paper (110,955, 100,000 read; the tail is probably references). Not read: MIRIX system prompts and services (the actual BM25 and embedding code), Memobase merge.py, post_process/profile.py, the pick_related_profiles prompt and the roleplay modul...
  - Code findings say what main did on 2026-11-01, not that it works. The MIRIX paper (v1, July 2025) predates the code I read (skills, auto-dream), so paper claims and current code may differ.
  - Cognee's report cites chunk and entity top-k of 20 while hybrid_retriever.py defaults to 5; evaluation overrides are plausible but I did not check.
  - VikingMem beats its own full-context run by 11 points on LoCoMo (88.83 versus 77.47) while MIRIX calls full-context the ceiling: either extraction-time normalisation (for example absolute dates) does real work, or the self-judged setup inflates scores; I cannot tell from the page.
  - LoCoMo category names: the Memobase counts 282, 321, 96 and 841 look like the dataset's categories 1 to 4, but I recall (asserted from memory, not verified here) that the dataset's own category-to-name mapping differs from the labels used in these tables, so do not draw conclusions from per-category labels without checking.
  - OpenViking's eleven type names come from a tree listing; only soul.yaml was read, so merge ops, scopes and semantics of the other types are unverified, and the README shows memory categories only by example paths.
  - Licences: OpenViking main project AGPLv3 (README); the Cognee and Supermemory licences are not stated in the README text I read. Check before any code reuse; the decision belongs to the owner.
  - 'No ablation' holds for the pages I read; OpenViking's blog report, Memobase docs or MIRIX's public_evaluation branch could contain more.
  - Test costs are my estimates for deepseek-flash within the lab's 1-4 USD band, not measured. The 30-call budget is used up, so conflicting numbers were not re-verified.
