# Mechanism synthesis protocol

Status: reviewed

## Why synthesis begins before global completeness

Waiting for “all papers” is impossible, while building from discovery snippets is unsafe. The project therefore uses repeated, reversible synthesis cycles. A cycle may narrow the next search or authorize a benchmark; it cannot declare universal completeness.

## Mechanism card

Every candidate must answer:

1. What observable failure does it address?
2. What is its input, state, update rule, and readout?
3. Which source directly supports the source-domain mechanism?
4. What does the source not establish?
5. What is the simplest engineering baseline?
6. What unique prediction distinguishes the mechanism?
7. Which benchmark category exposes its benefit and its characteristic failure?
8. What cost does it add in tokens, latency, disk, model calls, review, and privacy?
9. What result rejects, restricts, or supersedes it?
10. Can raw evidence and prior versions be recovered after it fails?

## Evidence-to-experiment threshold

A mechanism may enter an exploratory benchmark when:

- at least one primary source has been read with an exact locator;
- its computational abstraction is stated without biological identity claims;
- a simpler baseline and rejection test exist;
- a known failure case is included;
- the experiment does not require selecting the final architecture.

It may influence the provisional architecture only after triangulation, independent review, a registered experiment, a held-out gain, and acceptable safety/cost results.

## Synthesis cadence

Run a synthesis checkpoint when any condition occurs:

- 25 newly screened records in one topic;
- three newly primary-read decisive sources;
- a new contradiction or mechanism class;
- a baseline experiment completes;
- a prior conclusion fails reproduction;
- the topic reaches a coverage stopping-rule checkpoint.

Each checkpoint produces:

- supported, contested, rejected, and unresolved claims;
- mechanism cards changed since the prior checkpoint;
- benchmark implications;
- targeted next searches;
- an append-only memory event or supersession.

## Immediate sequence

1. Freeze and review `project-memory-lab-v0` questions and labels.
2. Run `no-memory`, `rg`, and FTS5 baselines.
3. In parallel, screen the Priority-A interdisciplinary query families.
4. Fully read three decisive sources each for complementary systems/replay, allocation/salience, semantic compression, prospective offloading, and crash consistency.
5. Hold Synthesis Checkpoint S1; choose at most two mechanisms for the next benchmark level.
6. Add local dense retrieval only after the lexical baseline artifacts are frozen.
7. Test the operational-salience vector only after consequence labels and adversarial cases exist.
8. Pilot an API screening worker only after a human/subscription workflow supplies a frozen comparison set.

## Definition of a potentially right direction

A direction becomes a serious architecture candidate when it improves at least two different task families, survives a characteristic adversarial case, preserves provenance and correction, remains useful across two reader-model families, and beats the simplest unlocked baseline under an equal retrieved-token budget. One positive benchmark score is not enough.
